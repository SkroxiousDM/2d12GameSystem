# 10. Conflict System

Use a scripted conflict for a consequential struggle in which opposing sides can make several meaningful tactical decisions. A conflict might be a fight, chase, argument, negotiation, infiltration, battle, supernatural contest, or struggle against a dangerous environment.

Each side declares an intent, establishes its starting Disposition, and secretly chooses three actions. Reveal and resolve those actions one volley at a time. Continue until one side wins, both sides are defeated, or the participants agree to a concession.

Disposition measures a side’s ability to keep pursuing its objective. It is not health, ammunition, morale alone, or a count of individual participants.

## 10.1 When to Use a Conflict

Use a scripted conflict when all three conditions are present:

- The outcome matters enough to deserve sustained attention.
- An opponent or significant force can actively resist the intended outcome.
- Several meaningful tactical choices could change how the struggle ends.

Otherwise, use an ordinary roll or resolve the situation without rolling.

A named opponent, a dangerous situation, or a large group does not automatically require a conflict. Choose the procedure that gives the situation appropriate attention.

### Each Side Needs an Objective

State what each side wants to accomplish, not merely what it wants to prevent.

Suitable objectives include:

- Hold the bridge until the refugees cross.
- Capture the courier before she reaches the harbour.
- Convince the council to release the prisoners.
- Escape the hunters without revealing the refuge.
- Reach shelter before the storm cuts off the pass.

An objective may be defensive. “Hold the bridge until the refugees cross” gives the defenders something concrete to achieve. “Stop the enemy” usually needs more definition.

Environmental opposition also needs a stated outcome: the storm strands the travellers, the fire consumes the archive, or the river carries away the crossing.

## 10.2 Setup

Complete these steps before scripting.

1. Declare intent and stakes.
2. Choose the conflict type.
3. Establish participants and sides.
4. Calculate starting Disposition.
5. Apply Superiority.
6. Record the adjusted starting Disposition.
7. Establish the acting order and script the first exchange.

### Intent and Stakes

Each side states what it gains by winning. Establish what losing allows the opposition to do and whether capture, death, permanent injury, exposure, or another lasting consequence is possible.

Discuss what withdrawal or concession could look like.

Intent limits victory. Defeating a side whose objective is to hold a doorway does not automatically let the winner kill everyone, seize unrelated possessions, or achieve another objective.

### Lethal Stakes

Establish lethal stakes before the roll or exchange that could produce a lethal outcome.

Stakes may change when circumstances change, but never retroactively. Explain the new danger and allow affected participants to respond before resolving it.

Lethal stakes make death possible. They do not make every successful Attack an injury or every defeat a death.

### Participants

A participant must be able to contribute to the side’s objective.

Helping to establish starting Disposition commits a character to that side’s stakes. Helping an action during the conflict likewise makes the helper a participant if they were not already involved.

A character who cannot meaningfully contribute may remain outside the conflict. Do not include them merely to increase Disposition.

### Starting Disposition

The leader of each side rolls:

2d12 + Disposition skill + highest Edge in that skill’s pool

Divide the result by three, rounding down.

Then:

- Add +1 per eligible helper.
- Apply flat starting-Disposition bonuses and penalties from abilities, creature rank, conditions, and similar effects.
- Apply a minimum of 1.
- Apply opposing Superiority.

Flat starting-Disposition modifiers have no stacking cap.

The adjusted result is the side’s starting Disposition for recovery limits and compromise.

Helping this setup roll grants the listed +1 Disposition per helper; it does not also grant the ordinary Help bonus die unless a specific rule says otherwise.

### Continuing the Same Conflict

Set fresh Disposition only for a genuinely new conflict with a materially different objective or changed circumstances.

Passing through a doorway, changing tactics, or turning a Fight into a Chase does not by itself begin a new conflict. Carry forward:

- Current and starting Disposition.
- Used injury trades.
- Taken Out participants.
- Existing injuries, Truths, and other continuing effects.

If the conflict type changes, establish the new action skills before the next exchange.

## 10.3 Superiority

Preparation can weaken an opposing side before the conflict begins.

Gain 1 Superiority for each applicable category, to a maximum of 3:

| Category | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Know Thy Foe | Discover an exploitable weakness, motive, fear, or vulnerability. |
| Know Thy Battlefield | Secure an advantageous position or understand circumstances that materially favour your approach. |
| Know Thy Players | Establish a useful plan, alliance, resource, or other preparation. |

Each category applies once. Preparation must materially affect the opposing side and the declared stakes.

Rephrasing one advantage does not qualify it for several categories. Additional categories require distinct material advantages.

After all other starting-Disposition modifiers and the minimum of 1, reduce the opposing side’s Disposition by your Superiority, to a minimum of 0.

Superiority is not tracked or spent after setup.

### Overwhelming Preparation

If Superiority reduces a side’s starting Disposition to 0, resolve the conflict as a victory for its opponent at no cost, within the declared intent and stakes.

Do not script exchanges or calculate a further compromise.

A low roll or negative flat modifiers alone cannot produce this result.

### Preparation and Other Benefits

An asset may retain its normal benefits while also contributing to Superiority.

A map might justify a preparation advantage and later help a relevant action. These benefits perform different jobs:

- Superiority represents preparation at setup.
- An action tag assists a particular roll.
- A Truth changes difficulty or establishes what is possible.

Apply each only when its requirements are met. One asset does not automatically provide all three.

## 10.4 Conflict Types

The conflict type determines which skills establish Disposition and resolve actions.

| Type | Disposition | Attack | Defend | Feint | Maneuver |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fight | Fight or Brawl | Fight / Brawl / Shoot | Athletics / Endure | Deceive | Notice / Stealth |
| Chase | Athletics or Drive/Pilot | Athletics / Drive | Athletics / Drive | Stealth | Notice / Survival |
| Argument | Persuade | Persuade | Will | Deceive | Empathy |
| Negotiation | Persuade | Persuade | Empathy | Deceive | Investigate |
| Infiltration | Stealth | Stealth / Tech | Deceive | Craft | Investigate |
| Battle | Lore (tactics) | Fight | Endure | Deceive | Lore |
| Spirit Duel | Arcana | Arcana | Will | Deceive | Lore |
| Hack / Netwar | Tech | Tech | Tech | Deceive | Investigate |
| Survive the Wild | Survival | Survival | Endure | — | Notice |

A dash means the action is unavailable.

Settings may add conflict types. Each needs a Disposition skill and a skill for every available action.

Use the listed skill that fits the action’s description. An action’s name does not remove the need for a plausible approach.

## 10.5 Scripting and Volleys

Each exchange contains three volleys.

Each side secretly scripts three actions in order, assigning an eligible participant to each. Both sides commit their scripts before revealing the first volley.

Reveal the corresponding actions together, describe them, and resolve their interaction. Repeat for the second and third volleys unless the conflict ends.

If the conflict continues, script another exchange.

### Coordination and Rotation

A side may appoint a coordinator to record its script and acting order. The coordinator does not have exclusive authority over the group’s decisions.

Track rotation continuously across exchanges:

- Every available participant must act before anyone takes another turn.
- A participant cannot act in consecutive volleys unless they are the only available participant.
- With more than three participants, continue the rotation into the next exchange.
- Helping does not count as taking a turn.

Update the rotation when someone joins, withdraws, or becomes unable to act.

Assigning an action that later proves unsuitable does not permit a free replacement.

### Changing a Script

Before revealing a volley, spend:

- 2 Momentum for the players’ side; or
- 3 Woe for the GM’s side.

Replace one unrevealed action. Keep its assigned actor unless an explicit ability allows reassignment.

### Action Interactions

| Your action | Attack | Defend | Feint | Maneuver |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Attack | Independent | Versus | Attack independent; Feint trumped | Versus |
| Defend | Versus | Independent | Defend trumped; Feint independent | Versus |
| Feint | Feint trumped; Attack independent | Feint independent; Defend trumped | Versus | Independent |
| Maneuver | Versus | Versus | Independent | Independent |

Independent actions both resolve, even if one defeats a side or takes its actor out.

A trumped action makes no roll and produces no effect. Describe the failed approach briefly.

### Independent Tests

Roll against Standard 15, adjusted by applicable Truths and other explicit difficulty modifiers.

Determine Power normally.

### Versus Tests

Both actors roll. The higher total wins.

The winner gains 1 Power, plus 1 for every full 3 points by which their total exceeds the opponent’s.

On an exact tie, neither action gains Power or produces its normal effect. Costs already paid remain spent. Resolve any applicable doubles complication.

Success at a Cost does not turn the losing versus roll into a successful action.

## 10.6 Actions and Effects

Describe how the chosen action pursues the side’s objective. The same action has different fictional expressions in different conflicts.

| Action | Purpose | Fight | Argument | Chase |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Attack | Advance the objective directly | Press an opening | Establish a decisive point | Close distance |
| Defend | Restore or protect your position | Regroup behind cover | Answer an objection | Recover a safe route |
| Feint | Exploit misdirection | Draw and bypass a response | Bait a revealing answer | Fake a route |
| Maneuver | Establish an advantage | Flank or obstruct | Shift the audience’s attention | Block a path |

### Attack

Remove opposing Disposition equal to the Power committed to this effect, plus 1.

An Attack need not represent bodily harm. It may drive an opponent back, expose a weakness, advance an argument, or bring pursuers closer.

### Defend

Restore 1 Disposition per Power committed, up to the side’s starting value.

Defend restores the side’s ability to pursue its objective. It does not heal statuses, restore Pools, repair equipment, or reverse an established consequence.

Against an opposing action in a versus test, winning also prevents that action’s normal effect.

### Feint

Remove opposing Disposition equal to the Power committed to this effect, plus 1.

Feint bypasses Defend but is trumped by Attack.

Against Maneuver, both actions resolve independently: the Feint may advance its objective while the Maneuver establishes an advantage.

### Maneuver

Spend Power on the following effects:

| Effect | Cost | Result |
|---|---:|---|
| Impede | 1 | The opposing side’s next acting participant takes −3 on their action roll. |
| Gain Position | 1 | Your side’s next acting participant gains one bonus die on their action roll. |
| Disarm | 3 | Disable a specific weapon, asset, or key tag for the remainder of the conflict. |
| Create a Truth | 2 per rank | Establish a justified Truth, applying explicit discounts and the normal minimum cost. |

Buy Impede, Gain Position, and Disarm no more than once each from a single Maneuver. They may be bought again through later successful actions.

Create a Truth may be purchased at a higher rank by paying its normal cost.

### Temporary Effects

Impede and Gain Position apply to the affected side’s next scripted action, including across an exchange boundary.

If that action is trumped or otherwise makes no roll, the effect expires unused.

They do not apply to unrelated Resistance or other incidental rolls.

Gain Position counts toward the normal bonus-dice cap.

### Disarming and Denying Options

Disarm must identify what is disabled and explain how.

Disabling one weapon or asset does not automatically remove an entire action category. Remove an action category only when the disabled asset was the side’s only plausible means of performing it.

A replacement asset may restore access when the fiction permits. Switching equipment does not itself change a script.

### Three Separate Layers

| Layer | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Disposition | The side’s ability to continue pursuing this conflict’s objective. |
| Pools | A character’s capacity for Effort, abilities, and Resistance. |
| Statuses | Lasting or temporary effects that have actually happened to a character. |

Losing one does not automatically reduce the others.

An ordinary Attack or Feint removes Disposition only. It does not also drain Pools or inflict a status.

Armour and Resistance do not reduce ordinary Disposition loss unless an explicit rule says otherwise.

## 10.7 Costs, Failures, and Complications

Conflict actions use normal Effort, Momentum, Woe, Tychē Points, tags, and Truths, subject to the restrictions below.

### Ordinary Failure

An ordinary failed action produces no action effect and generates the usual 1 Woe.

It does not also authorize an unrelated GM move, automatic injury, or additional Disposition loss.

The failed action and the opposing action already establish what happened in the volley.

### Success at a Cost

On an independent test that misses by 1–3, gain 1 Power as usual.

The player chooses:

- The GM gains 1 Woe; or
- Apply an immediate, proportionate complication arising from the action.

An ordinary cost cannot impose an additional status or extra Disposition loss unless an explicit rule permits it. Suitable costs include exposing an approach, losing access to a minor asset, or establishing a limited adverse fact.

Scenery actions use their listed Danger when the consequence option is chosen.

### Doubles

Apply critical Power normally.

Complications from doubles also apply. Keep them within the established stakes and the scale of the action. They do not cancel an independently resolving action or retroactively change the script.

Use the normal rules for buying off a complication with Woe.

### Explicit Risks and Harm

Abilities, hazards, Risky rolls, scenery Dangers, and other explicit rules may impose their stated consequences.

Resolve these separately from ordinary Disposition loss.

## 10.8 Scenery Maneuvers

A Maneuver may exploit an established feature of the scene.

The feature must be present as a Scene Truth or an established fact. If it has not been established, create or discover it first.

Name the feature when scripting the Maneuver.

If the feature is unavailable when the action resolves, the Maneuver produces no scenery effect. Do not substitute another feature for free.

Each feature supports one scenery action per conflict unless its nature clearly permits reuse.

| Action | Requirement | Power | Result | Danger |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Grab a Ride | A fixture and clear path | 2 | Gain Position and move to another reachable location in the scene; ignore Impede this volley. | Tier-1 winded. |
| Tip & Drop | A heavy object and a target below | 3 | Remove 3 opposing Disposition, without the Attack bonus. | An ally takes tier-1 struck. |
| Floor Collapse | A support that can be broken | 4 | Create a rank-2 Collapsing Floor Truth in the affected area. | Tier-1 stunned or a lost tag. |
| Carpet Pull | A suitable covering and unwarned foes | 2 | Apply Impede to each opposing participant’s next action roll this exchange. | Your next Defend takes −1. |
| Drape Drop | Hangings, netting, or similar material | 1 | Impede the opposing side’s next action. | Your next action takes −1. |
| Shove ’Em Off | An edge and an adjacent target | 3 | One opponent loses their next scripted action while recovering. | Tier-2 off balance. |
| Make a Hazard | Fuel, fluid, or a breakable container | 2 | Create a rank-1 hazardous Truth in the area. | You are also exposed to the Truth. |

The GM may trigger the listed Danger by spending 1 Woe.

Statuses imposed by a Danger are Resistable normally.

A fall is lethal only when lethal stakes have been established. Describe results consistently: a collapsing floor or spreading fire affects everyone its established scope reaches.

Dangerous Scene Truths generate Woe normally.

In other conflict types, scenery might be a witness, document, crowd, access point, market stall, or bridge. Adapt the nearest effect only when its requirements and consequences remain meaningful.

## 10.9 Shared Disposition and Participation

Each side has one shared Disposition total.

Do not divide it among participants. There are no individual Disposition shares or spill-over rules.

Ordinary Disposition loss does not remove a particular participant.

A participant stops acting when a status, explicit effect, voluntary withdrawal, or established circumstance prevents them from contributing to the objective.

Removing a participant does not directly reduce shared Disposition.

A side loses when:

- Its Disposition reaches 0; or
- None of its participants can continue pursuing its objective.

Finish independently resolving actions before checking victory.

## 10.10 Conflict Gear and Assets

Conflict equipment normally provides an action tag. It counts toward the normal +2 tag cap.

Equipment may also have one explicitly written quality that changes how it functions. Do not invent new qualities during resolution.

### Basic Action Tags

| Asset | Typical tag |
|---|---|
| Sword or knife | +1 Attack |
| Shield or prepared cover | +1 Defend |
| Spear, polearm, or scoped rifle | +1 Maneuver |
| Smoke grenade or false papers | +1 Feint |
| Relevant evidence | +1 Attack in an Argument |
| Prepared treaty draft | +1 Maneuver in a Negotiation |

An asset helps only when the description explains how it applies.

### Optional Distinctive Qualities

Use these qualities instead of treating every weapon as a different name for the same tag.

| Equipment | Quality |
|---|---|
| Sword | Before scripting the first exchange, assign its +1 tag to one action category for the conflict instead of always using Attack. |
| Axe | When at least 1 Power is committed to a successful Attack’s Disposition effect, remove 1 additional Disposition. Take −1 on Defend while using it. |
| Shield | Once per conflict, after a successful Defend, restore 1 additional Disposition. |
| Knife | Against a cumbersome weapon at established close range, Disarm costs 2 Power instead of 3. |
| Bow | While the opponent cannot reach you and lacks an effective ranged response, Attack versus Attack resolves as a versus test instead of independently. |

Additional Disposition loss is not additional Power.

A distinctive quality does not automatically provide a second tag.

### Non-Combat Assets

Evidence, promises, maps, supplies, local contacts, fortifications, and established plans can function as conflict assets.

Their benefits must follow their use:

- Evidence supports the point it actually establishes.
- A promise helps only if the recipient has reason to value it.
- A map helps only while its information remains relevant.
- Supplies and fortifications must have been secured before they can be used.

A player may explain an argument through direct dialogue or third-person description. Do not award a mechanical bonus merely for theatrical delivery.

### Changing Equipment

Declare active equipment before scripting each exchange.

Switching does not refresh a once-per-conflict quality, erase an established drawback, or restore a lost asset.

## 10.11 Accepting Injury

Once per character per conflict, a participant may accept a lasting injury to cancel some Disposition loss from a successful Attack or Feint.

Declare the trade before applying the loss.

| Accepted injury | Disposition loss cancelled |
|---|---:|
| Tier 2 | 1 |
| Tier 3 | 2 |
| Tier 4 | 3 |

Only one participant may make an injury trade against a single Attack or Feint.

Name the injury and explain how accepting it preserves the side’s position.

The injury must:

- Follow from the action.
- Have a relevant effect.
- Be new or worsen an existing status.
- Remain below tier 6 after stacking.

You cannot:

- Pay with an injury you already have.
- Use an irrelevant or wholly redundant status.
- Trade after being Taken Out.
- Cancel more loss than the action inflicted.
- Cancel loss from Maneuvers, hazards, voluntary costs, or special effects unless their rules allow it.
- Cancel an execution, imposed status, or outcome that is not ordinary Attack or Feint Disposition loss.
- Invent a bodily injury to pay for a purely logistical setback.

An accepted injury is a payment. Armour, Resistance, cancellation effects, and side benefits cannot prevent or reduce it.

It recovers through treatment and time.

The trade cancels incoming loss; it does not restore previously lost Disposition or increase the starting value.

Refresh it only when a genuinely new conflict begins.

## 10.12 Defeat, Mutual Defeat, and Concession

### Finish the Action

Complete an action’s costs, choices, and effects before removing a participant because of that action.

When actions resolve independently, both resolve even if one takes its opponent out.

Check victory afterward.

### Defeat

The winner achieves its intent within the established stakes, subject to compromise.

Do not require the defeated side to repeat the same unresolved contest. A new conflict requires a materially different objective or changed circumstances.

### Mutual Defeat

If both sides are defeated in the same volley, neither wins outright.

Resolve compatible parts of both intents and establish a shared setback within the stakes. Where the intents are incompatible, neither receives that incompatible outcome automatically.

Do not calculate a winner’s compromise.

### Concession

Before the next roll, a side may propose an end to the conflict.

State:

- What the opponent gains.
- What the conceding side retains.
- Any injuries, losses, promises, or obligations.
- How the immediate struggle ends.

The proposal must offer meaningful success and fit the situation. A mindless predator may require an escape opportunity or sacrifice rather than accepting surrender.

If accepted, apply the terms without another roll. Do not also apply compromise unless the agreement includes it.

Agreed costs cannot subsequently be Resisted or cancelled. Injuries recover normally.

If the proposal is rejected, continue the conflict.

Conceding grants no automatic Tychē Point.

## 10.13 Compromise

When one side wins a contested conflict, compare its remaining Disposition with its adjusted starting Disposition.

| Starting Disposition lost | Compromise |
|---|---|
| None | None |
| More than 0, but less than half | Minor |
| At least half, but less than three-quarters | Significant |
| Three-quarters or more | Major |

Use the proportions without rounding.

### What Compromise Means

The winner achieves its intent, but the loser earns a concession proportionate to how close the contest became.

| Grade | Appropriate outcome |
|---|---|
| Minor | A limited concession, delay, obligation, expenditure, or complication. |
| Significant | A substantial concession, restricted terms, important loss, serious injury, or meaningful part of the loser’s objective. |
| Major | A narrow victory at a major price: a lasting obligation, severe injury, important loss, or powerful new danger. |

These are packages of consequences, not mandatory lists.

An injury is not automatically added to an adequate non-injury concession.

Compromise cannot simply reverse the result or grant the loser its complete incompatible objective.

### Negotiating the Terms

1. Establish the compromise grade.
2. The loser proposes terms.
3. Confirm what the winner achieves.
4. Confirm what the loser retains or gains.
5. State all costs and who pays them.
6. Credit eligible existing injuries.
7. Resolve eligible Resistance.
8. Record the binding result.

Keep terms proportionate, relevant, and within the established stakes.

If agreement proves difficult, the GM adjudicates using those limits. Disagreement does not cancel the earned compromise.

### Injury Guidance

When an injury is an appropriate part of the package, use these tiers as starting guidance:

- Minor: tier 2.
- Significant: tier 3.
- Major: tier 4.

These are not automatic injuries, minimum costs, or penalties applied to everyone.

### Existing Injuries

An injury suffered during this conflict may satisfy one injury cost assigned to that character if its current tier equals or exceeds the required tier.

Each injury counts once.

Do not pool injuries, split them among costs, transfer them between characters, or credit them against non-injury consequences.

An injury treated below the required tier no longer satisfies that cost.

Do not add another injury merely because an existing one satisfies the first.

### Resistance

Imposed compromise statuses may be Resisted normally. Agreed payments cannot.

Resistance may reduce or remove an injury. It does not revoke the loser’s concession or another stated consequence.

Do not invent a replacement cost because Resistance removed an injury.

### Binding Results

The established victory, concessions, promises, and consequences stand.

They may be changed through genuinely new events and actions, but not ignored or immediately rerolled because someone dislikes the result.

## 10.14 NPCs and Harm

Ordinary NPCs and minion groups cannot accept injury trades.

A named rival built as a full PC may do so. Other NPCs require an explicit trait granting the option.

Ordinary NPCs have no Pools. They reduce imposed harm only through listed armour, traits, or abilities.

Apply at most one NPC reduction per imposed status application. It cannot reduce ordinary Disposition loss or an accepted payment.

Defeating an NPC side establishes defeat in this contest, not automatic injury or death. Resolve capture, escape, injury, or death through the stakes and final outcome.

Use a Dying clock for an NPC when rescue matters to play. Do not use it to make a rescue impossible after accepting rescue as an achievable objective.

Creature-specific rules remain in force. Immunity may prevent an unsuitable status without preventing Disposition loss in a contest the creature can lose.

## 10.15 Order of Resolution

1. Establish objectives, stakes, participants, and possible exits.
2. Calculate starting Disposition and apply Superiority.
3. Resolve overwhelming preparation immediately, if applicable.
4. Script the exchange and assign actors.
5. Reveal the next volley.
6. Determine the interaction.
7. Pay pre-roll costs and resolve running dry.
8. Roll and determine Power.
9. Make permitted post-roll choices and pay their costs.
10. Allocate Power.
11. Calculate Disposition loss separately from imposed statuses.
12. Offer eligible injury trades.
13. Apply remaining Disposition loss.
14. Resist eligible statuses, stack what remains, and resolve tier 6.
15. Complete any independently resolving opposing action.
16. Check removal, victory, and mutual defeat.
17. Before the next roll, allow concession proposals.
18. At the end, settle compromise or concession.
19. Recover Disposition, Pools, and statuses only through their respective procedures.

Specific rules override general rules only on the subject they address.

Healing a status does not also restore Disposition, refresh an injury trade, or remove a Scar.

## 10.16 Keeping It Fast

- Use full conflicts only when several tactical decisions matter.
- Record objectives and starting Disposition where everyone can see them.
- Use three cards or written entries for each script.
- Keep one continuous acting order for each side.
- Treat minion groups as a single coordinated participant unless their separation matters.
- Describe each volley briefly; reserve extended narration for decisive moments.
- Negotiate compromise as one coherent package rather than a list of accumulating penalties.

Disposition around 8–11 is the initial pacing target. Aim for two or three exchanges and roughly 15–20 minutes, then adjust through playtesting rather than adding procedural steps.
