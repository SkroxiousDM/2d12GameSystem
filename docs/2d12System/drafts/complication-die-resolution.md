# Complication Die Resolution — Design Draft

Status: Working design draft. This document records the current direction; it does not yet replace the published rules. Open questions below must be resolved before the coordinated rules patch.

## Stakes before the roll

Declare the goal and approach. Agree what Effects are needed to achieve the goal. The GM names the danger before the roll and sets the Difficulty. Do not introduce a new requirement after the roll.

## Working roll procedure

1. Roll two d12s, one designated as the Complication die.
2. Add both dice, Skill, and applicable roll modifiers. If the final total meets or exceeds the GM's Difficulty, the test succeeds; otherwise, it fails.
3. Compare the two unmodified die faces to determine the outcome.

| Die comparison | Test succeeds | Test fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complication die lower | Clean success | Failure with a benefit |
| Complication die higher | Success with a complication | Failure with a complication |
| Matching dice | Critical success | Critical failure |

The Complication die is always retained. It is one of the two action dice, contributes to the total, and is not a separate third die. Modifiers affect success or failure, not the die-face comparison.

Matching dice produce one critical outcome instead of the normal Complication die comparison. Explicitly triggered independent complications still resolve separately.

## Tags, modifiers, and Power

Relevant Tags provide Power rather than modifying the roll. Statuses and Conditions modify the roll. Keep the shared rule for Tag Power and tag compels in the shared rules rather than repeating it in every playbook.

A success with a complication generates the same Power as a clean success. The complication does not reduce the Power generated by the successful roll.

Power buys Effects; success does not grant an unfunded Effect. The exact Power formula and critical adjustments remain to be confirmed for this replacement procedure.

## Complications and severity

On a success with a complication, the GM assigns severity according to the danger named before the roll and its fictional impact. Preserve the successful roll's full generated Power.

| Severity | Power mitigation price | Woe buyoff price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor | 1 | 1 |
| Moderate | 2 | 2 |
| Major | 3 | 3 |

Treat multiple distinct complications independently. Assign each its own severity and its own Woe buyoff decision. Price each Woe buyoff against that complication's original severity, not its reduced severity.

## Power mitigation

Purchased Power may mitigate a complication before narrative Resistance. Enough Power must remain to fund the Effect being accomplished.

Partial mitigation is allowed: pay the difference between the original severity price and the reduced severity price. For mitigation pricing, removing a complication has a remaining price of 0. For example, reducing major to moderate costs 1 Power; reducing major to minor costs 2; removing major costs 3.

Purchased-Power mitigation may be applied to the same complication multiple times, subject to normal Effort limits and the requirement to fund the Effect. Resolve each separate complication independently.

## Narrative Resistance

Narrative Resistance reduces a complication by one severity band: major to moderate, moderate to minor, or minor to none. Apply Power mitigation before Resistance.

The procedure and resource cost for invoking Resistance still need to be specified in the integrated rules; this draft does not assume Resistance is free or repeatable without limit.

## Optional Risky die

Retain the optional Risky die as an independent complication trigger. It is distinct from the retained Complication die. A critical outcome does not suppress an independently triggered Risky complication.

Resolve a Risky complication separately, assigning its own severity and original-severity Woe buyoff price. Confirm the remaining Risky trigger, timing, and escalation wording during integration.

## Replacing Success at a Cost

Replace Success at a Cost with the six-outcome procedure above. Do not carry forward its reduced-Power result as the rule for success with a complication.

Where existing rules still refer to ordinary Success-at-a-Cost consequences during migration, treat those consequences as imposed, resistible consequences rather than protected payments. This does not make voluntarily paid action costs resistible.

## Open questions

1. Bonus dice: always retain the Complication die, but which other die is retained? Proposed, not yet approved: retain the highest other d12 and use those two dice for both the total and the outcome comparison.
2. What precise Power formula applies to successful tests, and what additional mechanical effects distinguish critical success and critical failure? Do not silently preserve old double-12 or double-1 exceptions.
3. What benefit does failure with a benefit provide, and who selects it? Do any failed outcomes generate Power or Weal?
4. What are the fictional bounds of each severity, especially on failed tests and in scripted conflicts?
5. What resource cost, fictional justification, and usage limit govern narrative Resistance? Where does a Woe buyoff decision occur relative to mitigation and Resistance?
6. How does this procedure apply to NPCs, versus tests, automatic successes, and abilities that manipulate dice or doubles?

## Coordinated integration checklist

- Replace the outcome table, doubles procedure, and Success-at-a-Cost wording in core resolution.
- Integrate Effort, purchased-Power mitigation, complication severity, and narrative Resistance.
- Update Woe buyoffs and optional Risky references.
- Reconcile scripted conflict, NPC, versus-test, and automatic-success procedures.
- Update quick reference, examples of play, and affected abilities.
- Recalculate bonus-dice odds after retention is settled; the existing keep-best-two odds must not be assumed valid for the proposed retention rule.
- Check cross-references and compile the affected rulebook after integration.
