# Complication Die Resolution — Design Draft

Status: Agreed working procedure, reconciled with the supplied conversation transcript and subsequent explicit decisions. This standalone draft does not yet replace the published rules. Use Difficulty throughout. Settled decisions below are not open design questions.

## 1. Establish the stakes

The player declares their goal and approach. Agree which Effects express the goal and how much Power is required to accomplish it. The GM sets the Difficulty and names the danger: what could go wrong. State unavoidable costs separately. If applicable, declare the action Risky and establish its rating.

The GM need not describe every possible severity before rolling. A named danger occurs only when a rule triggers it. Do not announce a new requirement after the roll.

## 2. Roll and retain dice

Roll two d12s, one designated as the Complication die. Roll any bonus dice alongside them, retaining the existing maximum of two bonus dice.

Always retain the Complication die and the highest single other action die. Discard the rest. Retention is mandatory: do not select a lower die to make a matching pair. Only the retained pair matters for matching dice. Use this same pair for the total and outcome comparison.

Add both retained dice, Skill, and applicable roll modifiers. A final total that meets or exceeds the Difficulty succeeds; a lower total fails. Tags provide Power rather than roll modifiers; statuses and Conditions modify the roll. Keep the shared Tag Power and tag-compel rules in the shared rules, not repeated in each playbook.

## 3. Determine the outcome

Compare the unmodified faces of the retained dice.

| Comparison | Total meets or exceeds Difficulty | Total below Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complication die lower | Clean Success | Failure with a Benefit |
| Complication die higher | Success with Complication | Failure with Complication |
| Matching dice | Critical Success | Critical Failure |

There is no separate miss-by-1–3 Success-at-a-Cost band. Every total below the Difficulty is a failure.

Matching dice replace the normal higher/lower comparison and produce one critical outcome, not a critical plus a comparison-based complication. Double 1s and double 12s have no special overrides. Independently triggered complications still occur.

## 4. Generate Power and determine consequences

### Successful tests

Normal success Power = Tag Power + 1 + floor((final total − Difficulty) / 3).

Each relevant tag supplies +1 Power, maximum +2 Tag Power per action. A tag must materially support the declared approach. Post-roll Effort may purchase additional Power under normal costs and limits.

Clean Success and Success with Complication generate the same Power. Power buys Effects; a successful roll does not grant an unfunded Effect or the declared goal by itself.

### Success with Complication

Generate normal success Power. The GM imposes a complication arising from the established danger and assigns minor, moderate, or major severity according to fictional impact. The complication cannot cancel or empty a purchased Effect.

### Critical Success

Generate normal success Power, then add +1 Power. Describe a flourish fitting the purchased result. The flourish grants no additional unpaid Effect or mechanical benefit. Matching dice generate no comparison-based complication; independent complications still resolve normally.

### Failure with a Benefit

The action fails and generates no Power, including no Tag Power. The GM names a useful fictional opening arising from the attempt and informed by the player's approach. It may reveal another route, clarify an obstacle, or create an opportunity, but does not accomplish the failed goal.

The opening grants no Power, Weal, Tych, numerical bonus, status tier, or Condition rating. Acting on it follows normal rules and does not guarantee success. Independent complications still occur and must be compatible with the opening.

### Failure with Complication

The action fails and generates no Power, including no Tag Power. The GM imposes a complication arising from the established danger and assigns its severity according to fictional impact. It is not automatically moderate or major. Reducing or cancelling it does not accomplish the failed goal.

### Critical Failure

The action fails and generates no Power, including no Tag Power. The GM imposes one major complication arising from the danger named before rolling. Its severity is fixed, but its consequences must fit the established fiction and stakes. A critical does not introduce otherwise unestablished death, permanent injury, or unrelated catastrophe.

The player may cancel this complication for 3 Woe before mitigation or Resistance, or Resist it normally. Power mitigation is normally unavailable because failure generates no Power and ordinary post-roll Effort requires success; another explicit rule may provide an exception. Reducing or cancelling the complication does not accomplish the failed goal.

## 5. Check the optional Risky die

For a Risky action, roll an additional, separately identifiable Risk d12 alongside the action dice. It does not contribute to the total, cannot be retained as an ordinary die, and does not count toward the bonus-dice limit.

| Rating | Independent complication triggers on |
| --- | --- |
| Risky 1 | 1 |
| Risky 2 | 1–2 |
| Risky 3 | 1–3 |

A Risky complication can occur on any outcome, including Critical Success. Assign its severity independently: Critical Failure does not automatically make the Risky complication major.

## 6. Name each complication

State each concrete consequence and its severity before the player chooses a response.

| Severity | Fictional impact | Woe buyoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor | Changes the next decision or creates a limited problem | 1 |
| Moderate | Significantly changes the scene or creates a substantial follow-up problem | 2 |
| Major | Dramatically escalates the situation or creates serious, lasting trouble | 3 |

Judge severity by impact in the current situation, not its name. A delay matters only when time matters; exposure matters only when someone can act on it. A complication cannot cancel or empty a purchased Effect.

Treat distinct complications independently. Each receives its own severity, buyoff decision, mitigation, and possible Resistance. Do not charge twice for different descriptions of the same consequence.

## 7. Choose how to respond

Resolve each complication in this order:

1. Choose whether to cancel it with an original-severity Woe buyoff.
2. If not, spend available surplus Power on mitigation, if desired.
3. Resist the remaining consequence once, if permitted and desired.
4. Resolve what remains.

Before the player commits resources, the GM explains what the reduction would change in the fiction.

### Woe buyoff

Before spending Power on mitigation or rolling Resistance against a complication, the player may cancel it by giving the GM Woe equal to its original severity: minor 1, moderate 2, major 3. This cancels the entire complication without consuming Power or reducing the purchased Effect.

Once Power mitigation or Resistance begins against that complication, its Woe buyoff is no longer available. Prices apply equally to ordinary Success-at-a-Cost consequences during migration, twists, and Risky complications.

### Power mitigation

Each Power spent reduces one complication by one severity band: major → moderate → minor → none. Partial mitigation is allowed. Reducing major to moderate costs 1 Power; major to minor costs 2; major to none costs 3.

Retain enough Power to fund the required Effect agreed before rolling. Only Power above that requirement may fund mitigation; do not reduce the required Effect to free Power. Surplus generated Power and purchased Power may both fund mitigation.

Multiple purchases may reduce the same complication, subject to normal Effort costs, timing, and limits. There is no separate mitigation cap. All mitigation shares the action's Power budget. A complication reduced to none does not occur.

### Narrative Resistance

After Power mitigation, the player may Resist a remaining imposed narrative complication once. Resistance is also available if no Power was spent first. Describe an appropriate response and choose the fitting pool. The GM states the reduced consequence before the player commits.

Resistance reduces severity by one band, including minor to none. Make the normal Resistance roll and pay its pool cost. The roll determines the cost, not whether the reduction occurs. Each distinct complication requires its own Resistance roll and payment and can be Resisted only once.

For an incoming harmful status, use Resistance's normal 2-tier reduction instead of a narrative severity-band reduction. Do not apply both from the same Resistance. Complication severity is not automatically status tier; major does not automatically mean tier-3 harm.

## 8. Resolve Effects and remaining consequences

Spend Power on the Effects the action accomplishes, preserving the required Effect when mitigating. Resolve remaining consequences. Removing a complication does not revoke a purchased Effect, create a replacement cost, award replacement Woe, or turn failure into success.

Resistance and mitigation address consequences as they unfold; they do not erase already-resolved events. Unavoidable costs and protected payments remain excluded, including explicitly accepted payments for benefits such as concession terms or an accepted injury trade.

Ordinary Success-at-a-Cost consequences during migration are imposed, resistible consequences, not protected payments. Choosing the consequence rather than its Woe buyoff does not change that.

## Example: mitigation and Resistance

An action generates 3 Power and requires 2 to achieve its goal. It triggers a major narrative complication. The player may first give the GM 3 Woe to cancel it. Instead, they decline the buyoff, reserve 2 Power for the Effect, and spend the surplus 1 Power to reduce major to moderate. They then Resist once, reducing moderate to minor and paying the rolled pool cost. Resolve the purchased Effect and remaining minor consequence.

The player could purchase additional Power under normal Effort rules to mitigate further before Resistance. Cancelling one complication would not cancel a separate Risky complication.

## 9. Scripted conflicts: settled versus procedure and limits

### Versus tests

Both actors roll, retaining their Complication die and highest other action die. Add Skill and applicable modifiers, then compare final totals. The higher total wins. Use the difference between the winning and losing totals as the winning margin, rather than a fixed Difficulty.

Winner's Power = Tag Power + 1 + floor((winning total − losing total) / 3).

Each actor compares their own retained, unmodified die faces:

| Comparison | Winner | Loser |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complication die lower | Clean Success | Failure with a Benefit |
| Complication die higher | Success with Complication | Failure with Complication |
| Matching dice | Critical Success: +1 Power | Critical Failure: one major complication |

The loser generates no Power, including no Tag Power. Reducing or cancelling the loser's complication does not turn their action into a success or prevent the winner's purchased Effects. Matching dice do not override a higher opposing total.

### Exact ties

An exact tie is a neutral stalemate. Neither actor wins, generates Power, or produces their action's normal Effect. Neither receives a comparison-based benefit, complication, or critical outcome. Costs already paid remain spent. Independently triggered complications, including a triggered Risky die, still resolve.

### Conflict boundaries

Winning a versus test still prevents the opposing action's normal Effect. Failure benefits remain purely fictional openings, not free mechanical purchases. Complications cannot duplicate an opposing purchased Effect, freely change the script, or cancel an independent action.

Comparison-based complications, including the major complication from Critical Failure, cannot impose additional Disposition loss or harmful statuses unless an explicit rule permits them. Severity does not itself grant that permission. A major complication remains serious within the established stakes and these limits; it does not automatically mean injury, immediate defeat, or loss of the conflict objective.

Independent hazards, Risky triggers, abilities, and other explicit rules retain only the consequence permissions they actually provide. Calling a complication independent does not grant additional permissions. Power mitigation and narrative Resistance address eligible complications, not an opponent's ordinary purchased Disposition effect.
