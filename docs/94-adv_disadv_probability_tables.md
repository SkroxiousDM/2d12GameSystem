# Advantage and Disadvantage Probability Tables

## Overview

Certain circumstances grant **advantage** or **disadvantage** on an action roll. In both cases, the **Complication die is always retained** — only the two non-Complication ("main") dice are affected:

- **Advantage:** Roll 1 extra d12 alongside the normal main die. Discard the **highest** of the two main-die results; keep the lower one plus the Complication die.
- **Disadvantage:** Roll 1 extra d12 alongside the normal main die. Discard the **lowest** of the two main-die results; keep the higher one plus the Complication die.

This means every advantage/disadvantage roll uses 3d12 total (2 main dice + 1 Complication die), for 12^3 = 1,728 equally likely ordered outcomes. The tables below are exact analytical results, not simulated.

## Reading the Tables

- **Advantage roughly doubles your Complication-favorable outcomes** at low-to-mid CTs. At CT 13, baseline success is 54.17%, advantage pushes it to 70.72% (+16.55 points), and disadvantage drops it to 37.62% (-16.55 points) — the swing is almost perfectly symmetric around the baseline at the midpoint CT.
- **Advantage suppresses Failure w/ Complication and boosts Success w/ Complication.** Because you're keeping the *lower* of two main dice, the Complication die is more likely to end up higher than the kept main die, shifting complications away from failures and toward the "succeed but..." zone.
- **Disadvantage does the mirror opposite.** Keeping the *higher* of two main dice makes the Complication die more likely to be lower than the kept die, pushing complications toward "fail with consequence" rather than "fail with minor benefit."
- **Critical rates shift asymmetrically, not just success rates.** At CT 13, Critical Success under advantage (108/1728 = 6.25%) is three times Critical Failure under disadvantage's mirrored position, because discarding a die changes how often the *kept* die can match the Complication die at all — matches become rarer under both advantage and disadvantage compared to a straight 2d12 roll, since you're selecting for extremes (min or max) rather than rolling the die pool at face value.
- **At the extreme CTs (2 and 24)**, advantage and disadvantage converge toward the same limiting behavior as baseline, since there's almost no room for the discard mechanic to matter when success is already near-impossible or near-guaranteed.

## Worked Example

At **CT = 13** with **advantage**: you roll a Complication die of 6, and two main dice showing 5 and 9. Discard the higher main result (9), keep the 5. Total = 5 + 6 = 11, which is <= 13, and since the Complication die (6) is higher than the kept main die (5), this resolves as a plain **Success**. Without advantage, if the 9 had been your only main die, the total would have been 15, a failure — advantage saved the roll by letting you keep the better of two attempts.
