The old world is buried beneath the present, but it has not fallen silent. This chapter covers the setting: Campaign Truths, day and night, vampires and the blood they need, the bled, dhampirs, how the afflicted die, old-world works, contracts and the long roads between towns.

> Every door in this valley opens for someone. Find out who, and you'll know who rules it.

## 1. The Setting in Brief

The old world is buried beneath the present, but it has not fallen silent. Its roads, foundations and sealed rooms are everywhere; a few of its works still run. No one agrees on when it fell, and every claim to rule rests on a history that cannot all be true.

Across the **Frontier** — wherever law, knowledge and working infrastructure give out — towns bargain with ancient households, physicians dispute the teachings of religious orders, and courts fight over succession, charters and the machines that keep people alive.

Among the living are **vampires**: people who must take blood to live. They are few, long-lived, hard to kill and often indispensable. Some claim to remember the world before the fall. Most rule nothing; some rule a great deal. Their **dhampir** children are rarer still.

**Tone:** grim, gothic, political. Locked rooms and disputed wills, blood ledgers and charity clinics, rifles beside silver knives, and monsters who know every family in town.

Players can be dhampirs (see the Focus *Carries Her Mother's Blood*) but not full vampires.

## 2. Campaign Truths

Agree on these at session zero. The first five are the **core Truths**. Replace or add to them from the optional list.

### 2.1 Core Truths

| \# | Truth | In play |
|----|----|----|
| 1 | **The Kept are few and indispensable** | Vampires are rare. A single vampire is a campaign arc, not a random encounter — and killing one rarely solves the problem it was part of. |
| 2 | **Blood behind every bargain** | Every vampire depends on a supply: a household, a levy or an exchange. The supply is politics. |
| 3 | **The Frontier pays for answers and fears them** | Towns hire investigators and Hunters because they must. They don't always want what's found. |
| 4 | **The old world's works are disputed** | Machines, miracles, curses and hauntings are argued over by physicians, priests and scholars. Science and Occult both apply (§3). |
| 5 | **Dhampirs are rare, feared and coveted** | Common folk fear them. Vampire households want them — as heirs, keys or worse. |

## 4. Day and Night

Time of day is always a **scene Truth**. Every scene has one.

| Time Truth | Effect |
|----|----|
| **Day** | Direct sunlight harms vampires and dhampirs (*Sun-Sick*, §7.1). Most vampires keep indoors or underground and many keep night hours, but they are not helpless by day: a vampire in a shuttered room is as dangerous at noon as at midnight. Their retainers, guardians and machines are active. |
| **Dusk** | One scene. The GM gains **+2 Woe** as the sun sets. Vampires come out. |
| **Night** | Vampires and dhampirs see in the dark and ignore darkness Truths. Scenes at night with a vampire present add **+1 Woe** at the start of the scene (dangerous Truth). The GM may use vampire Woe spends (§14). |
| **Dawn** | One scene. A vampire in the open must reach shelter or face the sun. At the end of the scene, any vampire still in direct light gains *Sun-Sick* at tier 2 at once, then normally. |

**Cloud and glass:** heavy cloud lessens the harm (§7.1). Experience says sunlight through a window does vampires little harm; no one agrees why.

### 4.1 The Dawn Clock

When the party must survive until morning, the GM sets a **Dawn clock** (usually 6 or 8 segments).

- It ticks **1 per scene** or **1 per conflict exchange**.

- Players may tick it with the **Advance** effect only by actions that waste time for the enemy (stalling, hiding, leading a chase in circles).

- When it fills, the scene becomes **Dawn**.

Use the Dawn clock as a **racing clock** against the vampire's own clock (*Breach the Village 8*, *Take the Girl 6*). Dawn doesn't destroy a vampire; it forces a choice: withdraw, or fight on while the sun takes its toll.

## 5. Vampires

### 5.1 Threat Ranks

Rank measures how dangerous a vampire is **now** — its resources, supply, allies and access to old works — not its age. A vampire who remembers the world before the fall can be any rank; one made last decade can be Paramount.

| Rank | Examples | Conflict Disposition bonus | Starting Woe when introduced |
|----|----|----|----|
| **Minor** | A starving fugitive, a household's newest vampire, a vampire kept as a prisoner | +0 | 1 |
| **Established** | Head of a small household, a physician with a private clinic | +2 | 3 |
| **Great** | Head of a great house, a vampire whose bloodline opens a town's treatment works | +4 | 5 |
| **Paramount** | A founder whose bloodline controls a region | +6 | 8 (and a danger clock that can end the campaign) |

### 5.2 Hard to Kill

Vampires survive wounds that would kill anyone else.

- A vampire's physical statuses **can't go above tier 5** unless the Attack uses a **killing method** (§8.1). At tier 5 the vampire is *broken*: it can still act, at −2 on physical rolls.

- **Recovery needs blood.** A wounded or broken vampire that rests a full day **and** takes at least half a litre of blood recovers all physical statuses. Without blood, it recovers only 1 tier per day.

- **Wounds make it hungry.** Each time a vampire is *broken*, tick its **Blood Debt clock** (§5.4) by 1.

- In a scripted conflict against a vampire, reducing its Disposition to 0 achieves the winning side's intent. If the intent is "destroy it", one PC must also make a **killing strike**: one roll vs. Hard 18 using a killing method. On a failure, the vampire escapes or recovers, and the compromise is at least Significant.

### 5.3 Vampire Traits

Each vampire has **3–5 power tags** and **at least 1 weakness tag**, for example: *the old hospital opens for me*, *a hundred donors*, *remembers the founding*, *faster than you can see*, *sees perfectly in the dark*; and *starving*, *the doors don't know me*, *obsessed with the past*, *proud beyond reason*, *my supply runs through one man*, *believes the old stories* (§8.1). Players can learn them with **Discover**.

**Pride:** the long-lived are proud. Once per scene, a PC may compel a vampire's pride (spend 1 Tychē Point or 1 Momentum): it accepts a duel, gives a warning before striking, or lets an insult distract it. The GM gains 1 Woe.

### 5.4 Blood Debt

Every vampire needs blood regularly. The GM tracks each vampire NPC's hunger on a **Blood Debt clock** of 6 segments, one segment per week since its last full feeding.

| Segments | What the players can see |
|----|----|
| 0–1 | Nothing. |
| 2–3 | Pallor, irritability, an unusual interest in blood (Notice, Empathy). |
| 4 | Pain, slowness, gifts failing: −1 on all its rolls. It takes risks to feed. |
| 5 | Desperate: −2 on all its rolls. Survival comes first; it may break its own rules. |
| 6 | **Husk**: the vampire dries out and goes dormant (§5.5). |

- Being *broken* ticks the clock (§5.2). A full feeding clears it. A partial feeding stops it ticking that week but doesn't clear it.

- **Animal blood** counts for half: a vampire needs twice as much of it.

### 5.5 Husks

Sometimes a starved vampire is found dried, light and rigid, like a corpse a century dead. Stories say such bodies can wake. Stories also say they burn like kindling.

- A husk is **unconscious and helpless**. Any killing strike against it succeeds automatically, and **fire destroys it at once**.

- Waking one takes about three people's worth of blood, absorbed over hours or days. It wakes at Blood Debt 5.

### 5.6 The Returned

Not everyone the Kept try to make becomes one of them. The making is dangerous and often fails, and most who fail die. A few survive as something else: the **returned**.

- **What people see:** the returned are strong and grow fast, and most die young, rarely past thirty. Many are slow, literal or strange in their thinking; how much varies. They don't need blood.

- **Not dhampirs:** folk often call them dhampirs, or cursed. They are neither. Dhampirs are born to vampire mothers (*Carries Her Mother's Blood*); the returned were made, and the making failed.

- **Evidence:** old-world doors and instruments sometimes react to one of the returned oddly, as if it half-belonged to a bloodline. No one agrees why. Households that make them would rather no one asked.

- **Levies:** some towns owe children under old charters. Rumour says the few who come back are the ones who failed.

- **NPCs only:** the returned are not a player option. A PC who survives a failed making leaves player control (§6).

## 6. The Bled

When a vampire feeds on someone — or anyone loses a great deal of blood — the victim gains the harmful status ***Bled***.

| Bled tier | Effect |
|----|----|
| **1** | A donation's worth. Pale and tired. No roll penalty; clears after a night's rest with food and water. |
| **2** | Light-headed: −1 on Sōma and Akmē rolls. |
| **3** | Weak: −2 on Sōma and Akmē rolls, and gain *Weary*. |
| **4** | Critical: the next harm of any kind Takes you Out. Needs a Medicine roll to stabilise. |
| **5** | Dying: Taken Out. Dies at the end of the scene unless stabilised (Medicine vs. Hard 18) or given a transfusion. |
| **6** | **Drained**. Dead — unless someone has both the reason and the means to make you one of them. |

- **Gaining tiers:** a measured feeding inflicts Bled-1. A hard feeding is an Attack (+2 or more). Stacking rules apply.

- **Resisting:** Resist with **Sōma** when the blood is taken.

- **Recovery:** 1 tier per full day of rest with food and water.

- **Transfusion:** with a willing donor and a transfusion kit, a Medicine roll vs. Standard 15 lowers Bled by 1 tier per Power. The donor gains Bled-1.

- **Frontier law:** where optional Truth 8 is in play, anyone known to be bled is examined, confined or exiled. Hiding the marks is a common compel.

- A PC who is Drained and made a vampire, or who survives a failed making as one of the returned (§5.6), leaves player control.

## 7. Afflicted Blood: Sunlight Syndrome and the Thirst

These rules apply to vampires and dhampirs (*Carries Her Mother's Blood*).

### 7.1 Sunlight Syndrome

Under the **Day** Truth, in direct sunlight, the afflicted gain ***Sun-Sick***:

| Condition                                         | Dhampirs | Vampires |
|---------------------------------------------------|----------|----------|
| Direct sun, at the start of each scene            | +1       | +2       |
| *Merciless Sun* (deserts, harsh sun)              | +2       | +3       |
| Heavy overcast, per scene                         | +0       | +1       |
| *Sunlit* room or *False Dawn* (§13), per exchange | +1       | +1       |

- Sun-Sick is a harmful status: it applies its tier as a penalty to physical rolls (Sōma and Akmē skills).

- Resist with **Sōma** as normal. A wide hat, a dark cloak or partial shade counts as **+1 Sōma Edge** for that Resist.

- While Sun-Sick is at tier 1+, abilities and rules that heal the afflicted (vampire recovery, *Mother's Blood*, *Dhampir Regeneration*) don't work.

- A vampire at Sun-Sick 4+ also gains *burning-1* each scene in the sun.

- **Recovery:** a full scene in shade or darkness lowers Sun-Sick by 2 tiers. At night it clears completely.

- **Tier 6:** collapse. Taken Out until carried into shade. A dhampir left for a whole day gains a Sōma Scar. A vampire left in direct sun for a full day is destroyed.

### 7.2 The Thirst

Dhampirs have a **Thirst** status, tier 0–6. It doesn't penalise rolls until tier 3. A dhampir has no weekly need for blood; the Thirst rises only from what happens to them.

| Trigger | Thirst |
|----|----|
| A scene with fresh blood spilled nearby (yours or someone else's) | +1 |
| You're Taken Out, or a pool drops to 2 or less | +1 |
| You use an ability marked *Thirst* in its entry | +1 (or as listed) |
| The GM compels the Thirst and you accept (gain a Tychē Point) | +1 |
| A full night of rest | −1 |
| Animal blood (a scene, no roll) | −1 |
| A cup or so of human blood — drunk, through broken skin or by transfusion (a scene, no roll) | −2 |
| Half a litre or more of human blood at once | Clear completely. If taken by force, gain a Pneuma Scar the first time (*I've tasted it*) |

| Thirst tier | Effect |
|----|----|
| **0–2** | No effect. |
| **3–4** | −1 on Pneuma rolls when blood is visible. Others notice you're hungry (Empathy, Notice). |
| **5** | In every scene with a bleeding human, Resist with Pneuma or move towards them. |
| **6** | **Blood Crisis**: you choose — feed from the nearest source this scene, **or** refuse, collapse (Taken Out) and gain a Pneuma Scar. Either way, Thirst drops to 3 afterwards. |

**Never feeding** is a Belief, not a rule. Blood Crisis keeps the choice, and the cost of refusing, with the player.

## 8. How the Afflicted Die, and Hunter's Gear

### 8.1 Killing Methods and Folklore

A **killing method** is a tag that lets an Attack take a vampire to **tier 6: destroyed** (§5.2).

| Method | Effect | Notes |
|----|----|----|
| **Beheading** | Killing method | The one sure way. The blade must be able to cut through (*heavy*, *fine*, or a dhampir's strength). |
| **Fire** (sustained) | Killing method against a vampire with a tier-4+ physical status; destroys a husk at once | A burning house, a funeral pyre. |
| **Sunlight** | No killing strike, but stops recovery and burns over time (§7.1) | A collapsed vampire left in direct sun for a full day is destroyed. |
| **Stake through the heart** | Not a killing method. Inflicts *staked*: the vampire can't act or recover while the stake stays in | Finish it with beheading or fire — or bargain with it while it's helpless. |
| **Silver** | Not a killing method. Wounds from *silvered* weapons can't be recovered until the next full day | An old custom. No one remembers why it works. |
| **Crosses, garlic, running water, invitations** | No physical effect | Folk practice. Some vampires believe in them anyway (below). |

**Believers:** a vampire may carry the weakness tag *believes the old stories*, or a specific one (*won't cross running water*, *can't bear the cross*). Against that vampire, the folk remedy works as a **compel**, not a law of nature: the vampire accepts it (the GM gains 1 Woe) or the GM spends 1 Woe to defy it.

Any personal weakness tag of a vampire can be **upgraded to a killing method** with 3 Power from Discover and Create effects, or a downtime clock (8 segments).

### 8.2 Currency and Wealth

The setting's currency is the **mark**. The 2d12 System doesn't track coins; use these story tags instead:

| Wealth tag        | Means                                          |
|-------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| *broke*           | Can't buy anything without a favour or a trade |
| *a few marks*     | A meal, a night at the inn, a box of bolts     |
| *a fat purse*     | A horse, a gun, a doctor's fee                 |
| *a lord's bounty* | A cyborg horse, an old-world weapon, a farm    |

### 9.2 Old-World Artifacts

**The Order of the Last Guns** (working name) was an order of armed charter-enforcers in a neighbouring province, destroyed there in a succession war. The Frontier sees only what it left behind: refugees, claimants and a few of its sidearms, which no one can make any more. Whoever carries one claims to speak for the old enforcing authority.

Old-world artifacts carry the standard quirk ***its owners will want it back***: it can be compelled for Tychē Points, and the GM may spend 1 Woe to make its old owners, or their heirs, notice it.

> **★ Old-World Longsword** — *Tags:* old-world steel, +1 Attack (conflict gear). *Ability:* counts as a killing method for beheading; ignores *Armour* on vampires. *Depletion:* none. *Quirk:* its owners will want it back.
>
> **★ Dimension Pocket** — *Tags:* holds more than it should. *Ability:* carry up to 3 extra load. Once per session, draw out an item you could plausibly have stored (no Momentum cost). *Depletion:* 1 in d12 (it opens somewhere else for a scene). *Quirk:* things inside come out wrong.
>
> **★ Cybernetic Eye** — *Tags:* sees in the dark, sees heat. *Ability:* once per scene, free Discover about anything in your line of sight. *Depletion:* 1 in d12 (blind in that eye for a scene). *Quirk:* its owners will want it back.
>
> **★ Order Sidearm** — *Tags:* old-world revolver, +1 Attack (range), recognised on sight. *Ability:* ignores *Armour* on vampires. Worn openly, it is a claim to speak for the Order of the Last Guns: a free bonus die on Persuade and Intimidate with people who remember the Order. *Locked:* many fire only for someone registered in the Order's armoury log; for anyone else such a gun is a heavy club. *Depletion:* 1 in d12 (the last proper cartridges are spent until more are found). *Quirk:* its owners will want it back — and so will every claimant.
>
> **Blood Locket** — *Tags:* holds a vampire's blood. *Ability:* that bloodline's doors and guardians half-recognise you (+1 die). *Depletion:* 1 in d12 (the blood spoils). *Quirk:* the bloodline's records note every door you open.

### 9.3 The Moving Lights

On a clear night, a few lights cross the sky faster than any star and never twinkle. They are old-world works, left overhead when the old world fell. Most are dead. A few still answer.

- **Under open sky only:** roofs, earth, deep canyons, thick forest and storms (*Sudden Storm*) cut them off.

- **Uses:** the Passenger can reach its bearer through them (*Carries a Passenger*), and a mind sent abroad can ride them (*Sends the Mind Abroad*). Some households keep old instruments that listen to them.

- **Overheard:** anything sent through the moving lights may be heard by whoever is listening.

- **Optional Truth 11** (§2.2, *The lights in the sky still move*): if it is in play, something up there is also watching.

## 10. Bounties, Commissions and Contracts

Most sessions start with a contract. The contract is the party's shared **Goal**. A contract is a **Bounty** (a quarry), a **Commission** (an investigation), an **Escort** or a **Recovery**.

### 10.1 Contract Sheet

| Element | Example |
|----|----|
| **Type** | Bounty |
| **Client** | The town council, on behalf of a farmer whose daughter was fed on |
| **Quarry** | Lord Varn, a vampire (rank: Established) |
| **Proof required** | His head, his blood ledger, or his signet ring |
| **Bounty** | *a lord's bounty* (a Great vampire: two) |
| **Deadline** | Before the levy is collected (danger clock, 6 segments) |
| **Complication** | Lord Varn insists he hasn't fed in five weeks — and a rival Hunter crew has the same contract |

### 10.2 Negotiating the Fee

Haggling with a client is a **Negotiation** conflict if it meets the conflict test (Core Rules §10.1); otherwise one Persuade roll vs. Standard 15.

- Each Power above 1 raises the fee by one step (*a fat purse* → *a lord's bounty*), or adds a perk (an advance, a horse, room and board, a share of the estate's salvage).

- A dhampir negotiating with humans faces the Truth *Feared and Despised* (+1 step Difficulty), unless they're already known and trusted. With a vampire household, they may face *Coveted* instead (§13).

### 10.3 Reputation

A Hunter's reputation is a **power tag** gained at milestones (*the Hunter who burned the Roth estate*). It counts as a tag on Persuade and Intimidate with Frontier folk. A **bad reputation** (*took the money and ran*) is a weakness tag that clients and rivals compel.

### 10.4 Rival Hunters

Rival crews use the **Faction Turn** rules (Module C.5) with a project clock on the same quarry. If their clock fills first, they claim the bounty, and the party's contract becomes a Disposition fight with the rivals (often a Negotiation or a Chase rather than a Fight).

### 10.5 Charter-Speech and Binding Instruments

Charters, testaments, compacts and levy agreements bind only if they are written or sworn in **charter-speech**: an archaic legal and liturgical form of the language. Notaries, clergy and record clerks can compose it, and so can some of the Kept. Most people can only recognise it.

- **Reading** an instrument in charter-speech is an Old Lore roll vs. Standard 15. **Composing** one correctly is Old Lore vs. Hard 18, or Standard 15 with a tag such as *fluent in the old pacts*.

- **Finding a flaw:** a Discover effect on an instrument (Old Lore or Investigate) can reveal an error in its charter-speech. The flaw becomes a tag, for example *the oath was sworn in the wrong form*.

- **Grounds, not a verdict:** a flaw is grounds to challenge the instrument. It does not void it. The challenge is settled by a Negotiation, a Duel of Wills or the politics around it, and the flaw is a tag the challenger can use there. Whoever benefits from the instrument will argue that the error doesn't matter.

## 11. Setting Conflict Types

These add to the conflict types in Core Rules §10.3. All use the standard scripting, Disposition and compromise rules.

| Type | Disposition | Attack | Defend | Feint | Maneuver |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|
| **Hunt** (track a vampire or monster to its lair) | Frontier or Investigate | Investigate / Frontier | Will / Endure | Deceive | Notice / Stealth |
| **Siege Until Dawn** (hold a town or camp until morning) | Old Lore (tactics) or Craft | Shoot / Fight | Craft / Endure | Deceive | Notice |
| **Duel of Wills** (an interrogation, an audience, a stare-down with something old and patient) | Will | Intimidate / Persuade | Will | Deceive | Empathy |
| **Estate Assault** (break into a household or old-world facility) | Science or Stealth | Science / Fight | Endure / Athletics | Stealth | Old Lore |
| **Chase** (setting note) | Ride or Athletics | Ride / Shoot | Ride | Stealth | Frontier |

### 11.1 Special Rules

- **Hunt:** the winner gets their intent *and* chooses the time of the confrontation (day or night). If the party wins with the quarry's lair found by day, the next scene starts with **Day**, and the quarry is shut in, away from the sun.

- **Siege Until Dawn:** run alongside the **Dawn clock** (§4.1). Each exchange ticks it by 1. If the Dawn clock fills before either side hits 0 Disposition, the vampire side must withdraw or face the sun: the defenders win with a compromise based on their remaining Disposition.

- **Duel of Wills:** there is no hypnosis; this is pressure, fear and bargaining. A PC who loses is **Taken Out** of the contest and must follow the vampire's intent for one scene, out of obligation, fear or a bargain struck. A vampire who loses withdraws or reveals something (lose 1 power tag for the session).

- **Estate Assault:** each exchange, the GM may spend 1 Woe to wake a guardian (reinforcement) instead of buying a bonus die. If an estate is taken by day, its vampire's Disposition in the next Fight starts −2.

## 12. The Frontier: Travel and Region Truths

### 12.1 Frontier Travel Notes

- **Cyborg horses** don't eat or tire: a party with them ignores the first Grind tick each leg. Normal horses need water: Supplies drop by 2 per leg in deserts.

## 15. Design Notes

- **Hard to Kill (§5.2)** keeps the two-stage fight — wear it down, then a killing strike — but ties recovery to blood. Wounding a vampire makes it hungrier, and a hungry vampire is a different problem. Watch how long this makes fights; if too long, allow the killing strike as part of the final Attack instead of a separate roll.

- **The Bled and the Thirst** are both statuses, so the existing Restore, Resist and compel rules work on them. Bled is plain blood loss; its tier 6 is death, with one ominous exception.

- **Day and night** no longer switch vampires on and off. Sunlight is a cost, not a cage; the daytime advantage comes from finding where a vampire is and getting light in.

- **Folklore** works through belief, not physics. The *believer* rule lets old remedies matter at the table without becoming true.

- **Sunlight Syndrome** still limits dhampirs by day, when the rest of the party is strongest. That trade is the core of the dhampir's role.

- **Science vs. Occult:** keep the line blurred on purpose. The guideline in §3 stops arguments without forcing a fixed answer.

- **Contracts as Goals** give the party a shared Goal each session, and personal Goals sit inside it.

- **The returned (§5.6)** are NPCs only. They give failed makings a face and a body of evidence without adding a second dhampir option.

- **Charter-speech (§10.5)** gives Old Lore a political edge: a flawed oath opens a dispute rather than ending one.
