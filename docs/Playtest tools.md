# PLAYTESTER'S PRIMER

Thank you for joining this test!

Testing is slightly different from playing a normal session. Here's everything you should know.

**How testing works.** Our goal is to see how the content holds up at the table. We'll play through the content, and then I'll lead a discussion about it, covering topics like:
- Did we understand the content and how to use it? Was everything communicated clearly?
- What ran smoothly? Were there any points of friction?
- Did anything seem too strong or too weak?
- Did the content feel cohesive? Were there holes in the story, jarring tonal swings, issues that broke immersion, etcetera?
- Was it fun?

I'll take notes during the test and the discussion, then use them to create a list of bugs that will guide revisions to the content. You can also take notes if you'd like, and if you do, I'd love to see them, but it's not a requirement.

**Our job is to identify issues, not develop solutions.** Just knowing that a specific element doesn't feel right is good enough. Sometimes a solution comes to us immediately and is uncontroversial and in that case it's worth noting, but we want to avoid getting bogged down trying to find solutions.
Ultimately, it's up to the designer to decide whether something is an issue, and if so, how to fix it.

**Build with the test in mind, but not for the test.** Certain options and tactics that are fine during a regular campaign cause problems in tests. If we're testing a monster, avoid using abilities that instantly kill or banish it, especially if there's only one of that monster on the field. We won't be able to collect data on it if it's removed from play before it can act.

Avoid choosing classes or builds that are especially "power-gamey." Builds advertised as especially strong or tough are not a great fit for the test because combat against those builds won't accurately reflect combat against most other builds.

One important difference between tests and campaigns is that in tests, you often start at full strength, with all of your resources, and reset to full strength after every combat. Avoid using this knowledge to your advantage. It's fine to go all out against a boss monster, but you should spend resources against non-bosses the same way you would in a regular campaign.

**Keep combat moving.** There's often a lot to get through in a single session, so every effort to know what you're going to do before your turn starts so you can act quickly. If something is unclear and the GM has to make a ruling, you can voice an objection, but don't let it turn into a lengthy discussion that eats into the play time. Last, avoid excessive flavor narration and role-playing, especially in combat, as it slows turns down.

**Don't have your fun at the expense of others.** You may be testing alongside people you've never played with before. Since you likely don't know the comfort levels of other players, avoid personal barbs against players or their characters, visceral descriptions of blood and gore, bigotry of any form, crude jokes, and harm to children, elders, and non-monstrous animals. Ask for permission before catching allies within harmful area-of-effect abilities. In the post-test discussion, don't talk over other players, and make sure that everyone has an opportunity to share their thoughts.
