## 1. The Setting in Brief

Ten thousand years from now, after nuclear war burned the old world, the **Nobility** — vampires who rebuilt civilisation with a science indistinguishable from sorcery — ruled humanity for millennia. Their empire is now in decline. The Capital has its own concerns; the **Frontier** is a scattering of fortified farm villages, desert trading posts, and ruins, where the remaining Nobles still descend from their castles to feed.

Humans survive on what little of the old science they kept, on fences of wood and garlic, and on **Hunters**: professionals who kill Nobles, mutants, and the monsters that Noble experiments left behind. Hunters are needed, paid, and feared. Dhampirs — half-human children of the Nobility — are the best Hunters and the most hated.

**Tone:** gothic horror on a post-apocalyptic frontier. Lone riders, dusty towns, moonlit castles, laser rifles beside wooden stakes, monsters with tragic histories, and Nobles who are beautiful, ancient, and bored.

**D himself** is best used as a rumour, a mirror, or a rare NPC, not as a player character. Players can be dhampirs (see the Focus *Carries the Blood of the Nobility*) without being D.

## 2. Campaign Truths

Agree on these at session zero. The first five are the **core Truths**; replace or add from the optional list.

### 2.1 Core Truths

| # | Truth | In play |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Nobility are dying, but not dead** | Nobles are rare, old, and terrible. A single Noble is a campaign arc, not a random encounter. |
| 2 | **Night belongs to the Nobility** | Noble powers work only after sunset (§4). Sunrise is the Hunter's best weapon. |
| 3 | **The Frontier pays Hunters and fears them** | Villages hire Hunters because they must. They don't want them to stay. |
| 4 | **Noble science is sorcery** | Machines, curses, genetic monsters, and weather control are the same thing. Arcana and Tech merge into the Occult and Science skills (§3). |
| 5 | **Half-blood is cursed blood** | Dhampirs are feared by humans and despised by Nobles. Sunlight hurts them; blood calls them. |

### 2.2 Optional Truths (d12)

| d12 | Truth |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The Sacred Ancestor still watches** — the first Noble left plans that are still unfolding. |
| 2 | **The Capital has forgotten the Frontier** — no help is coming from the Capital. |
| 3 | **Some Nobles want to end it** — certain Nobles seek death, love, or escape from their own kind. |
| 4 | **The machines still run** — Noble castles keep working with no master inside, defended by guardians nobody controls. |
| 5 | **The Barbarois remember their oath** — monster-blooded clans still serve the Nobility for pay or loyalty. |
| 6 | **Hunters compete for bounties** — rival crews race for every contract. Bounties go to whoever brings back the proof. |
| 7 | **The weather is a weapon** — Noble weather control still runs in places and still fails. |
| 8 | **The bitten are destroyed** — Frontier law demands that the Kissed be killed or exiled. |
| 9 | **The old faith is almost gone** — crosses still work, but nobody remembers why. |
| 10 | **Mutants are people too** — mutants live everywhere, and how they're treated is a frontier politics question. |
| 11 | **Something from outside is coming** — the ancient war against beings from beyond the stars is not quite over. |
| 12 | **The Hunters' Code** — Hunters keep a code: never abandon a contract, never hunt another Hunter's quarry. Breaking it makes you a target. |

## 4. Day and Night

Time of day is always a **scene Truth**. Every scene has one.

| Time Truth | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Day** | Noble powers don't work. Nobles sleep in coffins and are helpless unless woken. Their guardians, servants, and machines are still active. Dhampirs face Sunlight Syndrome (§7.1). |
| **Dusk** | One scene. The GM gains **+2 Threat** as the sun sets. Nobles wake at the end of this scene. |
| **Night** | Noble powers work. The GM may spend Threat on Noble night powers (§14). Scenes at night with a Noble present add **+1 Threat** at the start of the scene (dangerous Truth). |
| **Dawn** | One scene. Nobles must reach shelter or burn. Any Noble still in the open at the end of this scene takes *burning* at tier 3 at once, and +3 each exchange after that. |

### 4.1 The Dawn Clock

When the party must survive until morning, the GM sets a **Dawn clock** (usually 6 or 8 segments).

- It ticks **1 per scene** or **1 per conflict exchange**.
- Players may tick it with the **Advance** effect only by actions that waste time for the enemy (stalling, hiding, leading a chase in circles).
- When it fills, the scene becomes **Dawn**.

Use the Dawn clock as a **racing clock** against the Noble's own clock (*Breach the Village 8*, *Take the Girl 6*).

## 5. The Nobility

### 5.1 Noble Ranks

| Rank | Examples | Conflict Disposition bonus | Starting Threat when introduced |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lesser** | A turned human, a Noble's servant made into a vampire | +0 | 1 |
| **Noble** | A minor Noble of a decayed house | +2 | 3 |
| **Greater Noble** | A count, a duke, a feared warlord | +4 | 5 |
| **Ancient** | A survivor of the old empire's height | +6 | 8 (and a danger clock that can end the campaign) |

### 5.2 Undying

Nobles do not die of wounds. A Noble's physical statuses **can't go above tier 5** unless the Attack uses a **bane** (§8.1): a stake through the heart, beheading, sunlight, and so on.

- A Noble with a tier-5 physical status is *broken* but can still act at night, and recovers all physical statuses after a full day's sleep.
- An Attack with a bane tag can take the status to **tier 6: destroyed**.
- In a scripted conflict against a Noble, reducing its Disposition to 0 achieves the winning side's intent. If the intent is "destroy it", one PC must also make a **bane strike**: one roll vs. Hard 18 with a bane weapon. On a failure, the Noble escapes or recovers, and the compromise is at least Significant.

### 5.3 Noble Traits

Each Noble has **3–5 power tags** and **at least 1 weakness tag** beyond the standard banes, for example *cannot enter uninvited*, *obsessed with the past*, *proud beyond reason*, *hates mirrors*, *bound to its castle grounds*. Players can learn them with **Discover**.

**Noble pride:** Nobles are proud. Once per scene, a PC may compel a Noble's pride (spend 1 Fate Point or 1 Momentum): the Noble accepts a duel, gives a warning before striking, or lets an insult distract it. The GM gains 1 Threat.

## 6. The Kiss

When a Noble bites a human, the victim gains the status ***Kissed***. It works like any status but has special rules.

| Kissed tier | Effect |
|---|---|
| **1** | Two marks on the throat. Pale, tired, −1 on rolls in sunlight. |
| **2** | The **Call**: each night the Noble can spend 1 Threat to summon the victim. Resist with Spirit or obey. |
| **3** | Aversion to garlic and crosses (−1 when near them). The victim is a weakness tag for the party: *[name] can be called*. |
| **4** | The victim can't enter sunlight without becoming *burning-1* per scene. Their Instinct becomes "Protect my master's secrets." |
| **5** | The victim obeys the Noble's spoken orders. Medicine can no longer treat the Kiss. |
| **6** | **Turned**: the victim becomes a Lesser Noble. A PC who is Turned is lost to the GM unless the table agrees on a Dhampir-like transformation (GM's choice, campaign-level). |

- **Gaining tiers:** each bite adds tiers like any Attack (stacking rules apply). A Noble that feeds over several nights usually adds **+1 tier per night**.
- **Resisting:** the Kiss can be Resisted only with **Spirit** and only when the bite happens; it can't be Resisted afterwards.
- **Treatment:** a Medicine roll vs. Hard 18 (once per day) lowers the Kiss by 1 tier per Power, but never below 1 while the Noble lives.
- **Release:** destroying the Noble that gave the Kiss removes it completely, but a victim at tier 4+ gains a **Spirit Scar** (*marked by the Kiss*).
- **Frontier law:** in many villages (optional Truth 8), anyone known to be Kissed is exiled or killed. Hiding a Kiss is a common compel.

## 7. Noble Blood: Sunlight Syndrome and the Thirst

These rules apply to anyone with Noble blood: dhampirs (*Carries the Blood of the Nobility*), and some mutants and cyborgs built with Noble parts.

### 7.1 Sunlight Syndrome

Under the **Day** Truth, in direct sunlight with no shade, a character with Noble blood gains ***Sun-Sick***:

- **+1 tier** at the start of each scene outdoors in the sun (stacking normally), or **+2** in a desert or other place with harsh sun (Truth *Merciless Sun*).
- Sun-Sick is a harmful status: it applies its tier as a penalty to physical rolls (Vigor and Finesse skills).
- Resist with **Vigor** as normal. A wide hat, a dark cloak, or partial shade counts as **+1 Vigor Edge** for that Resist.
- **Recovery:** a full scene in shade or darkness lowers Sun-Sick by 2 tiers. At night it clears completely.
- **Tier 6:** collapse. Taken Out until carried into shade, and gain a Vigor Scar if left for a whole day.

### 7.2 The Thirst

Characters with Noble blood have a **Thirst** status, tier 0–6. It doesn't penalise rolls until tier 3.

| Trigger | Thirst |
|---|---|
| A scene with fresh blood spilled nearby (yours or someone else's) | +1 |
| You're Taken Out, or a pool drops to 2 or less | +1 |
| You use a ★ blood ability (marked *Thirst* in its entry) | +1 |
| The GM compels the Thirst and you accept (gain a Fate Point) | +1 |
| A full night of rest | −1 |
| Drinking animal blood or blood from a willing donor (a scene, no roll) | −2 |
| Drinking from an unwilling human | Clear completely. Gain a Spirit Scar the first time (*I've tasted it*) |

| Thirst tier | Effect |
|---|---|
| **0–2** | No effect. |
| **3–4** | −1 on Spirit rolls when blood is visible. Others notice you're hungry (Empathy, Notice). |
| **5** | Every scene with a bleeding human, Resist with Spirit or move toward them. |
| **6** | **Bloodlust**: the GM controls your next action. Afterwards, Thirst drops to 3 and you gain a Spirit Scar. |

**Never feeding** is a Belief, not a rule. A dhampir who never drinks human blood is still a dhampir; the Thirst exists so the table can play that struggle.

## 8. Banes and Hunter's Gear

### 8.1 Banes

A **bane** is a tag that can destroy a Noble (§5.2). Standard banes:

| Bane | Works on | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Stake through the heart** | All Nobles | Wood, or an alloy that mimics it. The classic finishing blow. |
| **Beheading** | All Nobles | The blade must be able to cut a Noble (*Noble-forged*, *fine*, or a dhampir's strength). |
| **Sunlight** | All Nobles | Leaving a Noble in the open at Dawn counts as a bane strike. |
| **Fire** (sustained) | Lesser Nobles; others while *broken* | A burning castle, a funeral pyre. |
| **Crosses and garlic** | Lesser Nobles, weak Nobles | They impose Truths (*Warded*) rather than destroying. Greater Nobles laugh at them. |
| **Running water** | Some Nobles (GM sets per Noble) | A Truth that a Noble can't cross: *the river runs between us*. |

Any personal weakness tag of a Noble can be **upgraded to a bane** with 3 Power from Discover and Create effects, or a downtime clock (8 segments).

### 8.2 Currency and Wealth

The setting's currency is the **dala**. The 2d12 System doesn't track coins; use these story tags instead:

| Wealth tag | Means |
|---|---|
| *broke* | Can't buy anything without a favour or a trade |
| *a few dalas* | A meal, a night at the inn, a box of bolts |
| *a fat purse* | A horse, a gun, a doctor's fee |
| *a Noble's bounty* | A cyborg horse, a Noble weapon, a farm |

### 8.3 Setting Gear

Weapons and gear use the equipment rules in **playbooks.md** §2 (load, gear as tags, armour). These are common across playbooks:

| Load | Item | Tags |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Wooden stake, belt knife, flint striker, water skin | *stake* (bane) |
| 1 | Garlic bundle and a cross | *Warded* (create the Truth for one scene against Lesser Nobles; 1 Power) |
| 1 | Steel neck guard | *Armour 1* against bites only; the Kiss gains 1 fewer tier |
| 1 | Crossbow and wooden bolts | *+1 Attack* (range), *bolts are stakes* (bane at range, Hard 18) |
| 1 | Revolver or rifle | *+1 Attack* (range) |
| 2 | Laser rifle (rare, Capital-made) | *+1 Attack* (range), *burns through armour*; depletion 1 in d12 (power cell) |
| 1 | Long cloak and wide hat | +1 Vigor Edge when Resisting Sun-Sick; *+1 Feint* in the dark |
| 1 | Flares and sun-lamps | Create *False Dawn* for one exchange (2 Power). Lesser Nobles can't attack while it lasts |
| 0 | Cyborg horse (vehicle) | *tireless*, *fast as the wind*; not on foot missions. Treat as a story tag when absent |
| 0 | Normal horse or mule (vehicle) | *sure-footed*; ticks the Grind clock 1 less per leg |

### 9.2 Noble Artifacts

Noble artifacts carry the standard quirk ***the Nobility will want it back***: it can be compelled for Fate Points, and the GM may spend 1 Threat to make a Noble or its servants notice it.

> **★ Noble Longsword** — *Tags:* Noble-forged, +1 Attack (conflict gear). *Ability:* counts as a bane for beheading; ignores *Armour* on Nobles. *Depletion:* none. *Quirk:* the Nobility will want it back.
>
> **★ Dimension Pocket** — *Tags:* holds more than it should. *Ability:* carry up to 3 extra load. Once per session, draw out an item you could plausibly have stored (no Momentum cost). *Depletion:* 1 in d12 (it opens somewhere else for a scene). *Quirk:* things inside come out wrong.
>
> **★ Cybernetic Eye** — *Tags:* sees in the dark, sees heat. *Ability:* once per scene, free Discover about anything in your line of sight. *Depletion:* 1 in d12 (blind in that eye for a scene). *Quirk:* the Nobility will want it back.
>
> **Guardian Collar** — *Tags:* protected from the Call. *Ability:* +1 tier reduction when Resisting the Kiss or the Call. *Depletion:* 1 in d12 (it hums and burns: *burned-1*). *Quirk:* tells the Noble who made it where you are.

## 10. Bounties and Contracts

Most sessions start with a contract. The contract is the party's shared **Goal**.

### 10.1 Contract Sheet

| Element | Example |
|---|---|
| **Client** | The mayor of Ransylva, on behalf of a farmer whose daughter was bitten |
| **Quarry** | Count Varnas, a Noble (rank: Noble) |
| **Proof required** | His head, his heart, or his signet ring |
| **Bounty** | *a Noble's bounty* (a Greater Noble: two) |
| **Deadline** | Before the girl reaches Kissed-6 (danger clock, 6 segments) |
| **Complication** | A rival Hunter crew has the same contract |

### 10.2 Negotiating the Fee

Haggling with a client is a **Negotiation** conflict if it meets the conflict test (proposal §10.1); otherwise one Persuade roll vs. Standard 15.

- Each Power above 1 raises the fee by one step (*a fat purse* → *a Noble's bounty*), or adds a perk (an advance, a horse, room and board, a share of the castle's salvage).
- A dhampir negotiating with humans faces the Truth *Feared and Despised* (+1 step Difficulty), unless they're already known and trusted.

### 10.3 Reputation

A Hunter's reputation is a **power tag** gained at milestones (*the Hunter who burned Castle Roth*). It counts as a tag on Persuade and Intimidate with Frontier folk. A **bad reputation** (*took the money and ran*) is a weakness tag that clients and rivals compel.

### 10.4 Rival Hunters

Rival crews use the **Faction Turn** rules (Module C.5) with a project clock on the same quarry. If their clock fills first, they claim the bounty, and the party's contract becomes a Disposition fight with the rivals (often a Negotiation or a Chase rather than a Fight).

## 11. Setting Conflict Types

These add to the conflict types in proposal §10.3. All use the standard scripting, Disposition, and compromise rules.

| Type | Disposition | Attack | Defend | Feint | Maneuver |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hunt** (track a Noble or monster to its lair) | Frontier or Investigate | Investigate / Frontier | Will / Endure | Deceive | Notice / Stealth |
| **Siege of the Night** (hold a village or camp until dawn) | Noble Lore (tactics) or Craft | Shoot / Fight | Craft / Endure | Deceive | Notice |
| **Duel of Wills** (a Noble's gaze, hypnotism, the Call) | Will | Intimidate / Occult | Will | Deceive | Empathy |
| **Castle Assault** (break into a Noble's castle) | Science or Stealth | Science / Fight | Endure / Athletics | Stealth | Noble Lore |
| **Chase** (setting note) | Ride or Athletics | Ride / Shoot | Ride | Stealth | Frontier |

### 11.1 Special Rules

- **Hunt:** the winner gets their intent *and* chooses the time of the confrontation (day or night). If the party wins with the quarry's lair found by day, the next scene starts with **Day** and the quarry asleep.
- **Siege of the Night:** run alongside the **Dawn clock** (§4.1). Each exchange ticks it by 1. If the Dawn clock fills before either side hits 0 Disposition, the Noble side must withdraw: the defenders win with a compromise based on their remaining Disposition.
- **Duel of Wills:** a PC who loses is **Taken Out** of the will contest and must follow the Noble's intent for one scene (or gains Kissed-1 if the intent was to feed). A Noble who loses must withdraw or show its true face (lose 1 power tag for the session).
- **Castle Assault:** each exchange, the GM may spend 1 Threat to wake a guardian (reinforcement) instead of buying a bonus die. If a castle is taken during the day, its Noble's Disposition in the next Fight starts −2.

## 12. The Frontier: Travel and Region Truths

Use **Module B — The Grind & Travel**. The Frontier is harsh: most Grind clocks are **6**, deserts and Noble-poisoned lands are **4**.

### 12.1 Frontier Travel Notes

- The Guide rolls **Ride** or **Frontier**.
- **Cyborg horses** don't eat or tire: a party with them ignores the first Grind tick each leg. Normal horses need water: Supplies drop by 2 per leg in deserts.
- **Night travel** halves Grind clock size but every encounter is at Night.

## 15. Design Notes

- **Undying (§5.2)** is the main change from the core rules. It means a Noble fight has two stages: wear it down, then land a bane strike. Watch how long this makes fights; if too long, allow a bane strike as part of the final Attack instead of a separate roll.
- **The Kiss and the Thirst** are both statuses so that the existing Restore, Resist, and compel rules work on them. Neither one reduces rolls until mid-tier, so they create tension before they create penalties.
- **Sunlight Syndrome** limits dhampirs during the day, which is when the rest of the party is strongest. That trade is the core of the dhampir's role.
- **Science vs. Occult:** keep the line blurred on purpose. The guideline in §3 stops arguments without forcing a fixed answer.
- **Bounties as Goals** give the party a shared Goal each session, and personal Goals sit inside it.
