## 7.1 Boss Phases

Boss Phases mark changes in a boss’s tactics as its side loses ground. Phase changes cost no Woe; using a revealed Special or calling reinforcements still costs the listed Woe. These phase mechanics remain proposed unless separately approved below.

### Approved eligibility: phased-boss designation

When preparing an adversary, the GM may mark a Great or Paramount adversary as a Phased Boss. Rank alone does not grant Boss Phases. Rank, the Leader role and phase eligibility are separate: a powerful adversary need not have phases, and a Leader need not be a phased boss.

Before calculating starting Disposition, designate which eligible adversary, if any, is the phased boss for each side. Record that designation on its card or the conflict record. A side may have no more than one designated phased boss in a conflict. Establish the designation before seeing the setup result, not in response to an unexpectedly low Disposition total.

The designation is fixed for that conflict. It cannot be added, transferred or replaced after setup, including when reinforcements arrive or the designated boss leaves.

A minion group, Minor or Established adversary cannot receive the designation unless an explicit rule grants an exception. A full-PC rival likewise requires an explicit phase-capable entry; do not infer eligibility solely from its fictional importance.

If setup ends in overwhelming preparation or simultaneous setup defeat, do not activate Boss Phases (Core Rules §10.3).

#### Multiple bosses on one side

Other Great or Paramount adversaries on the same side retain their normal ratings, Role traits and Specials, but receive no benefits from the designated boss’s phases unless a phase expressly affects them.

Phase thresholds use the designated boss’s side’s shared Disposition. They are not personal tracks and do not create separate Disposition totals for the other adversaries.

Do not split a coordinated side merely to obtain additional phase systems. Separate sides require genuinely distinct objectives and independent participation.

#### Departure and return

If the designated boss becomes unable to participate, its designation remains recorded but cannot pass to another adversary.

Designation alone grants no permission to act while Taken Out, return to a conflict or use phase benefits while unable to contribute. If another rule legally returns the boss to the same unresolved conflict, it retains its designation and recorded phase history; setup and used phase triggers do not reset.

The exact activation and resumption of individual phase benefits remain for the separate participation ruling. This paragraph settles ownership without pre-empting that decision.

#### Limits of designation

Marking an adversary as a Phased Boss does not itself increase Grit, starting Disposition, Threat or Woe; grant another turn, script change or reinforcement; add unlisted Specials; or make the adversary immune to defeat, removal or finishing procedures. It does not establish that the encounter cost is balanced.

Any additional benefits must come from the separately approved phase procedure. Their numerical impact remains a playtest question. Recording this eligibility decision does not activate the phase proposal in the rulebook.

### Approved Special availability

Owned means a Special is listed on the card. Available means its phase and other prerequisites permit use. Revealed means the players have learned about it. Revealing a locked Special through a Tell, observation or an information effect does not unlock it.

#### Ordinary activated Specials

Ordinary activated Specials are phase-locked. Assign each owned ordinary Special to Start or Half using this table:

| Number of ordinary Specials | Available at Start | Newly unlocked at Half |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 0 |
| 2 | 1 | 1 |
| 3 | 1 | 2 |
| 4 | 2 | 2 |

The Half column is newly unlocked Specials, not the total available afterward. All ordinary Specials allocated by the table are unlocked after Half; Desperate unlocks no additional Specials under this schedule.

Record the specific assignments before calculating starting Disposition. They are fixed for the conflict and cannot be moved in response to setup results, scripts or changing circumstances. A locked Special cannot be used even when its other prerequisites are met.

Start-assigned Specials are available when the conflict proceeds beyond setup. Half-assigned Specials become usable from the next volley after Half activates, including a later volley in the same exchange. If Half activates after volley 3, the first opportunity is the next exchange's first volley. The Special's own declaration timing and the committed action must still permit use.

#### Passive Specials

Passive Specials operate from Start, subject to their own listed conditions. They do not require a Half unlock.

#### Defeat-triggered Specials

Defeat-triggered Specials unlock and are revealed when Half activates. Like other Half-unlocked Specials, they become usable from the next volley.

If defeat occurs before they become usable, including during a volley that would otherwise qualify the boss for Half, they cannot trigger. A locked Special cannot activate retroactively after its trigger has passed. This availability rule does not authorise activating Half after the conflict ends or invent a new victory-check order.

#### Allocation and activation boundaries

The allocation table counts ordinary activated Specials only. Passive and defeat-triggered Specials follow their separate Start/Half rules. All still count toward the card's normal Special limit unless an existing rule expressly excludes them. This records the counting convention used in the consolidated ruling, not an increase to the Rank allowance.

Unlocking or revealing does not activate a Special. Its listed trigger, action requirement, Woe cost, targets and restrictions still apply. No unlock grants an extra action, free script change, actor reassignment, permission to act while Taken Out or a suitable approach that was otherwise absent.

Half may unlock an owned reinforcement Special assigned to that phase. It does not grant a new reinforcement Special, summon reinforcements automatically or waive costs and arrival procedures. This does not change current/starting Disposition accounting.

The separate Last Stand, Fail-safe and compromise-rider replacements remain unapproved proposals. Unlocking an inherited Special does not resolve its incomplete timing or package-credit wording.

### Fixed phase thresholds — proposed

At the end of conflict setup, after all modifiers and Superiority, record the boss side’s shared Disposition as its phase reference. Record two thresholds:

- Half: half the phase reference, rounded down.
- Desperate: one third of the phase reference, rounded down.

The phase reference is fixed for this conflict. It is not the boss’s personal Grit or a separate Disposition track. If Superiority resolves the conflict at setup, do not activate Boss Phases.

### Checking phases — proposed

Resolve the volley’s actions, then check removal and victory normally. If the conflict continues and the boss remains a participant, activate any untriggered phase whose threshold the side’s current shared Disposition is at or below.

Each phase activates once per conflict. If both thresholds are met in the same check, activate Half first, then Desperate. A phase grants no immediate extra action or free script change. The approved next-volley timing above governs newly unlocked Specials; other phase-benefit timing remains a separate decision.

| Phase | Trigger | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | The conflict continues after setup | The boss begins with its Role trait, passive Specials and Start-assigned ordinary Specials, subject to their conditions. Show a clear Tell. Special availability is approved; other phase mechanics remain proposed. |
| Half | Shared Disposition is at or below the Half threshold | Unlock Half-assigned ordinary Specials using the approved allocation; unlock and reveal defeat-triggered Specials. They become usable next volley. Any reinforcement option must be owned and paid for normally. A Leader’s minions benefit from Command only while that Leader remains active; this phase grants no additional +1. |
| Desperate | Shared Disposition is at or below the Desperate threshold | Proposed: the boss’s Best action gains +2 to its rating, and Defend uses its Weak rating. Show a desperate tactic, offer a deal or reveal an exit. Desperate unlocks no additional Specials under the approved allocation. |

### Recovery and reinforcements — approved accounting boundary

Recovery and reinforcements do not reverse an activated phase, allow it to activate again or change either phase threshold.

Reinforcements add their Grit to current shared Disposition only, even above the side’s starting value. Starting Disposition remains the normal recovery cap and compromise reference. The phase reference and its thresholds remain fixed at their setup values.

Removing a participant does not, by itself, subtract its Grit or change any of these references.

### Taking out the boss or Leader — proposed phase handling

A Taken Out boss cannot activate a new phase or use phase benefits to act unless another rule explicitly permits it. Previously activated phases remain recorded; they do not restore the boss to participation.

Command’s minion-rating bonus and reduced script-change cost end when the Leader is Taken Out or otherwise leaves the conflict. Boss Phases do not reactivate Command.

Record a new phase reference only when a genuinely new conflict begins. Changing location, conflict type or tactics within the same unresolved struggle does not reset the reference or activated phases.

Eligibility, Special availability and reinforcement accounting are recorded decisions, not approval of the entire phase system. Desperate Defend/Best-action precedence and numerical benefits, final-total checks versus threshold crossings and low reference values, timing of non-Special phase benefits, and detailed participation/resumption rules remain separate decisions. The Last Stand/Fail-safe and compromise-rider package remains a separate proposal.
