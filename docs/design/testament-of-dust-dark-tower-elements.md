## 1. Ground Rules for Borrowing

### 1.1 Why the Dark Tower fits

The *Dark Tower* shares Hollow World's core premise. It is a decaying world full of old machinery that people treat as relics, holy objects, or monsters. Authority there rests on lineage and on artefacts nobody can make any more. Roland's revolvers, for example, are said to be made from the metal of a legendary sword, and they work as a sigil "recognized throughout Mid-World" ([Wikipedia — Roland Deschain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Deschain)). The old machines were built by a company whose maker's mark turns up across the ruins ([Dark Tower Wiki — North Central Positronics](https://darktower.fandom.com/wiki/North_Central_Positronics)). Its vampires, its rules about who gets to rule, and its frontier towns all suit the Vesper March.

### 1.2 What it may contribute

Following §3.5 of the design document (originality guardrails), King's work is used as a **source of structures, situations, and atmosphere**. It does not supply names, institutions, or named concepts. Each borrowing below has to pass four tests:

| Test | Requirement | Design-doc rule |
|---|---|---|
| Material | It has a mechanism the GM can explain. No sorcery and no fate | F1, F3, F6 |
| Plural | It doesn't need one origin that explains the whole world | F4, V12, P6 |
| Political | It produces a dispute over legitimacy, not just a monster | P1, I1, I2 |
| Original | It can be expressed without King's names or terms | §3.5 |

### 1.3 Major conflicts to avoid

- **The Tower and the Beams.** A single cosmic structure that holds up reality is the Continuance problem all over again (§12.1): one origin that explains too much. **Do not import.**
- **Doors and other worlds.** Travel between worlds breaks the material, single-Earth foundation. **Do not import.**
- **Ka as literal fate.** Destiny that actually steers events breaks F3 (belief does not change physics). **Import only as a belief people hold** (D13).
- **The Crimson King and his servants.** A single overarching dark lord breaks "No clean hands" and P6. **Do not import.**

## 3. Detailed Adaptations

### D1. The Mercy That Feeds **[PROPOSED]**

**Source.** In "The Little Sisters of Eluria," wounded travellers are taken into a hospital tent run by nuns. The nuns heal them with tiny bug-like "doctors," then feed on them once they recover. A medallion taken from a dead man protects its wearer ([Wikipedia — The Little Sisters of Eluria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Sisters_of_Eluria)).

**Why it fits.** This is the House of Mercy's internal problem (§8.1), "its blood service supports arrangements some members condemn," pushed to its gothic extreme. It's also an Exchange (§4.8) where the payment is your recovery.

**Hollow World version**

- A travelling or roadside nursing order with a vampire at its centre. Patients are treated well and genuinely healed. Their convalescence is long, and their blood is drawn while they recover.
- **The wound-cleaners.** The order uses cultured larvae and leeches to clean wounds. This is real, material medicine. Folklore mixes it up with vampirism ("the Sisters' little helpers drink you"). It's a *mistaken explanation* (§5.10). These are not nanites and are not related to them.
- **The protective token.** Patients wearing a particular token are never fed on. The cause is a **local custom / compact**, not physics: the order's founding agreement forbids feeding on registered members of a sister house. The token is a membership mark. It can be forged, sold, or taken from a corpse.

**Three layers (I1)**

| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| Apparent event | Travellers recover at the mercy-house and then waste away; survivors speak of kindness |
| Material mechanism | Long-term drawing of blood under sedation, kept below the level that kills; some patients are kept on as permanent donors |
| Political consequence | The order runs the only clinic for three days in any direction. Closing it kills more people than it feeds on |

**Seed.** A PC wakes up in the mercy-house wearing a token that isn't theirs. The patient in the next bed recognises it as his dead brother's.

### D2. The Long Levy **[PROPOSED seed — mechanism CANON (Q37)]**

**Source.** In *Wolves of the Calla*, masked raiders come once a generation and take one child from each pair of twins. Months later the children come back "roont": mentally damaged, destined to grow huge and die young ([Wikipedia — Wolves of the Calla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Tower_V:_Wolves_of_the_Calla)). The Breakers' enhancing pills are later revealed to be made from the stolen children's brain matter ([Dark Tower Wiki — Breakers](https://darktower.fandom.com/wiki/Breakers)).

**Why it fits.** It's a Levy (§4.8) at the scale of generations. It also offers a home for the **alternative dhampir origin** the design document set aside in §5.12: "survivors of a failed conversion."

**Hollow World version**

- Once a generation, riders from beyond the March collect a fixed number of children from certain towns. The obligation is written into the towns' founding charters, in return for old protections: water rights, defence, or a treatment work's output.
- Most children never come back. A few return months later, changed.
- **GM-only mechanism (option A) [CANON — Q37]:** the riders' household is trying to make clean-tagged heirs on working conversion apparatus (§5.7, §5.9). Children are used because the household believes young recipients survive conversion better. The ones who come back are **failed conversions**: survivors carrying a partial nanite population that never integrated. They are unusually strong, fast-growing, and short-lived, and their thinking is impaired.
- **~~GM-only mechanism (option B)~~ [RETIRED — Q37]:** the children are kept as long-term donors with a specific blood quality, and the returned ones are those whose blood failed the test.
- No mask, no wolves, no twins. The riders' identity markers and the selection rule should be original (birth-order, birth-month, or a census mark).

**Three layers (I1)**

| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| Apparent event | Monsters take the children; the returned ones are cursed |
| Material mechanism | Conversion trials (A) or donor selection (B), conducted under a charter |
| Political consequence | Refusing the levy voids the charter, and with it the town's legal claim to its water and its protector |

**Safety note (§3.6).** This seed involves harm to children. Flag it in the lines-and-veils discussion. It works fine with the taking and the harm kept off-screen.

**Ruling (Q37) [CANON].** Returnees are **not dhampirs**. They are a separate, NPC-only failed-conversion condition. Folklore calls them dhampirs or "the returned." Each carries a degraded fragment of the converting lineage's identity tag, so a returnee is walking evidence of which household runs the Levy. See design document §5.12.

### D3. The Output Sold Beyond the Border **[PROPOSED]**

**Source.** In *Wizard and Glass*, the remote barony of Mejis hides a working old oil field. Its output is being moved toward the rebel enemy, and Roland's group ends up blowing the field up ([Reactor — Wizard and Glass, "Reaping"](https://reactormag.com/a-read-of-the-dark-tower-constant-reader-tackles-wizard-and-glass-qcome-reapq-chapter-9-reaping/)).

**Why it fits.** Hollow World has already established that "some of [the House of Mercy's] collected blood leaves the March under contract" (§8.2). King's version adds the **young outsiders sent to a quiet backwater** who discover that the backwater is a supply depot for someone's war.

**Hollow World version**

- A sleepy border town keeps an old pumping station, cold store, or treatment work going. It looks like a minor local asset.
- In fact, a convoy is being assembled of preserved blood, purified water, or reconditioned old-world parts, bound for a power at war elsewhere. That power is supplying a vampire army or a besieged court.
- The PCs come as a minor commission (a survey, an audit, an escort) and work it out from the ledgers (§4.8 "blood records as evidence").

**Political consequence.** Destroying the depot starves an army, and starves the town too. Exposing it embarrasses whoever signed the contract, and that person may be the PCs' patron.

### D4. The Order of the Last Guns **[CANON placement — Q40]**

**Source.** Roland's revolvers are heirlooms made from the metal of the sword of his line's founder. They are recognised on sight and serve as his identification ([Wikipedia — Roland Deschain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Deschain); [TV Tropes — The Dark Tower](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Literature/TheDarkTower)). His order fell, and he outlived it.

**Why it fits.** It is the "surviving relic, no supply chain" principle (§4.5) applied to legitimacy. Holding the weapon means holding the claim.

**Hollow World version**

- **[CANON — Q40]** It belongs to a **neighbouring province**. The Vesper March receives only claimants, refugees, and scattered sidearms.
- A **regional, extinct** order of armed officers. (There is no universal hunters' guild, §7.5.) It once enforced old-world charters in a particular province. Its officers carried old-world sidearms that nobody can now make or fully maintain.
- The order was destroyed in a succession war. Its remaining weapons are scattered, and each one is now **evidence**: a charter carried in a holster. Whoever bears one can claim to speak for the old enforcing authority.
- **GM-only.** Some of the sidearms carry their own biometric locks, separate from the nanite tags (plural origins, F4). They fire only for people registered with the order's armoury. The registration log still exists somewhere (compare tag logs, V25).
- Ties in with existing material: the Truth Generator's "A famous Hunter didn't die here" (B.12) and the Artefact quirk ***its owners will want it back*** (B.9).

**Seeds**

- Two claimants to a lordship each produce one of the order's guns. Only one of them will fire.
- A dhampir PC finds that one sidearm half-responds to them. Why would an enforcer's weapon recognise a vampire lineage?

### D5. The Mad Machines **[PROPOSED]**

**Sources**

- **The riddling train.** Blaine, an insane, suicidal monorail, forces passengers into a riddling contest to save their lives ([Dark Tower Wiki — Blaine the Mono](https://darktower.fandom.com/wiki/Blaine_the_Mono)).
- **The guardian bear.** Shardik is a giant cyborg bear guardian. Tribes once revered it by another name, and when it is found it is decaying, worm-infested, fevered, and attacking strangers it mistakes for old enemies ([Dark Tower Wiki — Shardik](https://darktower.fandom.com/wiki/Shardik)).
- **The servant machine.** The helpful robot of the farming town is a product of the same old-world maker ([Dark Tower Wiki — North Central Positronics](https://darktower.fandom.com/wiki/North_Central_Positronics)).

**Why it fits.** Digital Facsimiles (§5.15) are already confident, articulate, incomplete, and out of date. King shows how to make a machine like that frightening, sad, or funny.

**Hollow World versions**

| Adaptation | Description | Canon check |
|---|---|---|
| **The route-voice** | A Digital Facsimile that lives in an old transit system: a cable line, a rail car, a lock-and-canal network. It is "hardware-bound," but the hardware runs along a fixed route, so it travels without leaving its machinery. It was built from a long-dead operator's records and won't accept that its timetable is centuries out of date | Fits Q21 (bound to hardware) |
| **The catechism** | A **Duel of Wills** variant (B.11) against a machine. It demands correct answers in an outdated protocol: old names, old procedures, old jokes. Remembered NPCs can help, at a price | Uses B.11 unchanged |
| **The worshipped sentinel** | A guardian unit at a treatment work, revered by a nearby community as an animal-spirit. It is breaking down and infested, and it attacks strangers it mistakes for intruders logged decades ago | Truth table: *mistaken explanation* (§5.10) |
| **The obliging servant** | A household machine that is helpful, polite, and well loved, and that reports everything to whoever holds its ownership record. The town doesn't know who that is | Fits §4.1 "operating exactly as intended … whose interests" |
| **The maker's mark** | One old-world manufacturer's mark shows up on many unrelated devices across the March. Some sects treat it as a holy sigil. It's **one maker among many**, not a world-spanning conspiracy | Fits F4 (keep it plural) |

**Seed.** A route-voice in a canal lock has been holding back flood water for three centuries. It wants a passenger to answer one question correctly before it opens. The last person who knew the answer was a Remembered vampire who has every reason not to come.

### D6. The Rite of the Recorded Beat **[PROPOSED]**

**Source.** In the dying city of Lud, one faction descends from the original scientist-defenders. They have become a ritualised society that kills one of its own whenever the "god-drums" play. The drums are a loop of an old rock song's drum track ([Dark Tower Wiki — Lud](https://darktower.fandom.com/wiki/Lud)).

**Why it fits.** It's Wolfe's "correct procedure for the wrong reasons" (§3.3) turned inside out: a wrong procedure set off by a real mechanism. It is also pure *preserved procedure / mistaken explanation*.

**Hollow World version**

- In a decaying city district, an old public-address system on a failing timer sometimes plays a recorded tone sequence. It was once an evacuation or quarantine alert.
- The district's ruling faction holds that the sound demands a **lottery**: one person leaves, is exiled, or is offered to the district's vampire.
- **GM-only.** The original alert meant "quarantine this sector." The lottery comes from an outbreak in which the infected were sent out. The district's vampire has encouraged the rite for generations because it supplies them (*deliberate deception*, §5.10).

**Political consequence.** Whoever silences the speakers ends the rite and wrecks the faction's legitimacy, and the vampire loses a source of supply. Leaving it alone is also a choice.

### D7. The Burning of the Straw Dead **[PROPOSED]**

**Source.** At Mejis's Reap Fair, straw effigies, the "stuffy guys," are burned on a bonfire. The custom replaces an older practice of human sacrifice, and the original meaning of its name has been forgotten ([Dark Tower Wiki — Charyou Tree](https://darktower.fandom.com/wiki/Charyou_Tree)).

**Why it fits.** Hollow World already has the folklore "Burning the dry dead" (§4.10) and the canon that husks are extra flammable (V15). This turns it into a festival.

**Hollow World version**

- A harvest or year's-end festival in which stuffed effigies are dressed, carried through town, and burned.
- **Origin (GM-only).** Communities used to burn husks found among the graves (§5.8). Over time the practice became an effigy rite. Some families still say "burning the dry" without knowing what it means.
- **The festival is camouflage.** Every so often, an effigy has a real husk inside it. A rival is disposed of in public, with the whole town cheering.

**Seed.** The PCs need a particular husk revived (§9.1 "is the landholder actually dead?"), and the festival is tomorrow night. One of the effigies is too heavy.

### D8. The Hunter's Taxonomy **[CANON handout — Q41]**

**Source.** In *Wolves of the Calla*, the priest Callahan sorts vampires into types. Type Ones are rare, long-lived, can hibernate for generations, and can make new vampires. Type Twos are made vampires that are short-lived and dim. Type Threes are "like mosquitos" ([Reactor — Wolves of the Calla, "The Priest's Tale"](https://reactormag.com/a-read-of-the-dark-tower-constant-reader-tackles-wolves-of-the-calla-part-2-qtelling-talesq-chapter-3-qthe-priests-tale-new-yorkq-sections-1-9/)). Type Threes leave their victims dazed and forgetful ([Dark Tower Wiki — Vampires](https://darktower.fandom.com/wiki/Vampires)).

**Why it fits.** It's the best possible example of F5, "folklore can be wrong about mechanism and right about observation." A hunter's field guide is a great player-facing handout, as long as it's wrong in interesting ways.

**Hollow World version.** An in-world hunters' classification. Each class is accurate about what hunters observed and wrong about why:

| In-world class | What hunters observe | What's actually going on (GM-only) |
|---|---|---|
| **Winterers** | Ancient, cunning, vanish for decades, come back terribly hungry | Husk dormancy (§5.8) and revival debt; often Remembered, but not always |
| **Strays** | Newly made, clumsy, short-lived, hunted down fast | Improvised conversions with poor control and poor supply; they die because nobody maintains them, not because they are a lesser kind |
| **Tapsters** | Feed lightly, leave donors confused, rarely kill | Well-supplied, restrained vampires taking partial feedings (§5.5); the confusion comes from sedatives in a household, not from the bite |

**Use [CANON — Q41].** The handout goes in the **Stalker** chapter and is cross-referenced in the **Occultist** chapter. The classes are **Winterers**, **Strays** and **Tapsters**. Give players the field guide as a handout. Let investigation pull it apart. Threat ranks (V37) stay as the GM's actual measure.

### D9. The Kept Specialists **[PROPOSED]**

**Source.** In the final books, captive psychics, the Breakers, are held in a comfortable compound and fed enhancing drugs so they keep working against the Beams ([Dark Tower Wiki — Breakers](https://darktower.fandom.com/wiki/Breakers)).

**Why it fits.** Technical authority (§4.9) means controlling the people who can read logs, run treatment works, or interpret facsimiles. A gilded cage is the natural way to hold them.

**Hollow World version**

- A household keeps a compound of **readers**: people who can interpret tag logs (§5.9), operate conversion apparatus, or talk with a speaking saint. They have good food, libraries, and their families nearby. They cannot leave.
- Some don't know what their work is for. Some know and stay anyway.
- Hooks: a commission to "recover" a reader who has fled (are the PCs rescuers or bounty hunters?); a reader who quietly falsifies log readings as sabotage.

**Q30 link.** This is where psychic-flavoured Focuses can find a material basis. See §4.

### D10. The Man of Many Names **[PROPOSED]**

**Source.** Roland's adversary, the man in black, recurs under several names and guises across the saga.

**Why it fits.** Age is contested (§4.7), the Remembered are unreliable witnesses, and tag logs can prove or disprove a lineage (V25).

**Hollow World version**

- A figure turns up in chronicles across four centuries under different names: an advisor, a physician, a heretic, a broker. Every appearance comes just before a succession crisis.
- **GM decides** whether this is one Remembered vampire, a lineage of cleanly converted descendants who pass for the founder at the door (§5.9 impostors), a title several people have held, or a historians' fabrication.
- The tag logs could settle it, and getting at them is a full investigation (Q24).

### D11. The Town That Turns **[PROPOSED]**

**Source.** In *The Gunslinger*, the town of Tull rises against Roland when its preacher incites the townsfolk to kill him ([Stephen King Wiki — Tull](https://stephenking.fandom.com/wiki/Tull)).

**Hollow World version.** A preacher or healer who serves a hidden vampire (or is the vampire's attendant, §4.10 "the faithful attendant") turns a whole town against the party in a single sermon. The horror is intimate (§3.4 rule 3): these are people the PCs drank with last night. Use the **Siege Until Dawn** conflict (B.11) with the crowd as the threat. Mechanically it's a Woe clock (§7.7). Morally, every person the PCs kill is a neighbour.

**Tone guardrail.** King's version ends in a massacre. In Hollow World, give players a way out that costs them something (rule 1, "grim, not nihilistic").

### D12. The Tunnel Folk **[PROPOSED]**

**Source.** Glowing mutants in the tunnels under the mountains; the mutant "Green Folk" who attack Roland at Eluria ([Stephen King — Little Sisters of Eluria](https://stephenking.com/darktower/book/little_sisters_of_eluria_(short_story).html)).

**Hollow World version.** Communities living in old transit tunnels and service galleries, altered by old-poisoned ground (B.12). Treat them as **people with their own politics**, not as a monster type. They suit Mutant playbook backgrounds (§7.8), and they're the people who know the tunnels between treatment works. Avoid King's slur-like terms.

### D13. The Formal Tongue and the Sworn Company **[Register CANON — Q39; sworn companies PROPOSED]**

**Source.** Mid-World's split between a formal "High Speech" and everyday speech, its distinctive dialect, and the sworn, fate-bound company (the ka-tet).

**Hollow World version**

- **A formal legal register [CANON — P7, Q39].** Charters, wills, and blood compacts must be written or spoken in an archaic formal register to be binding. Vesper March working name: *charter-speech*. Few people can compose it correctly. An error in the register is grounds to challenge a testament; whether that voids it is itself the dispute (directly useful for §9.1 *The Second Testament*). The Remembered may speak it natively, or claim to.
- **Sworn companies.** A party can formally swear itself as a company under local custom. The oath has legal weight (shared debts, shared liability, shared claim to bounties) and social weight. Some people believe it binds their fates. It doesn't (F3), but believers act as if it does. This is a natural formal name for the **Blood Compacts / Passage Houses** material in §12.4.
- Build the vocabulary from scratch. Don't adapt King's words (see §5).

---

### D14. The Qualifying Trial **[PROPOSED]**

**Source.** A young gunslinger earns his guns by choosing a weapon and defeating his teacher in single combat.

**Hollow World version.** Wardens and other bound offices (§7.8) qualify by defeating or outwitting their predecessor or examiner, using a weapon or method they choose. It's a good **background event** for a Belief or Instinct (Tychē, §7.7): "I chose the wrong weapon and won anyway."

---

### D15. Drifting Distances **[PROPOSED — optional]**

**Source.** In Mid-World, directions and distances are no longer reliable.

**Hollow World version (material only).** The Survey Office's old records contradict each other (§8.1). Also, some old-world waymarks, beacons, and road signals still broadcast outdated or deliberately false routes. Caravans following "the old markers" end up in the wrong valley. **Space never really changes** (F3). Maps are just wrong, and some are wrong on purpose.

---

### D16. Tone: Elegy and the Consuming Quest **[PROPOSED]**

- **Elegy.** King writes decay with grief, not only with grit. Rule addition (§3.4): *the past was not better, but it was more, and people miss it.* Every ruin was somebody's ordinary workplace.
- **The consuming quest.** Roland gives up companions for his goal. In Hollow World, a PC's Goal can be written as something that **demands** sacrifices, and Tychē (§7.7) can reward taking them. Woe can escalate when a character refuses. Make the cost visible and leave the choice with the player.

## 4. Contribution to Q30 (Psychic and Possession Focuses)

The design document lists Q30 as open: *Sends the Mind Abroad* and *Carries a Passenger* need a material basis. The Dark Tower's captive psychics and mad machines suggest these options:

| Focus | Option | Material basis | Canon check |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Sends the Mind Abroad* | **A. The Relay-Sense [CANON — Q30]** | An inherited or surgically installed old-world implant (Cyborg Hunter adjacent) that picks up nearby sensor and relay networks: cameras, door logs, facility speakers. The character "sees far" only where the old networks still run | Keeps F1 and F3; the limits are geographic and investigable |
| | **B. The Trained Trance** | Not supernatural. Old procedures of memory and observation (a monastery practice) plus a sedative preparation, producing remarkable deduction and recall that people experience as visions | Purely human; weaker but safe |
| *Carries a Passenger* | ~~A. The Hosted Facsimile~~ | A Digital Facsimile stored in an implant or carried relic that talks only to the bearer | **Rejected (Q38):** no portable facsimiles |
| | **B. The Tethered Voice [CANON — required by Q38]** | An earpiece relic linked to a facsimile at a fixed site. It works only within range of that site's relays, so the passenger "goes quiet" far from home | Fully consistent with Q21 |

**Status.** Q38 rules out option A for *Carries a Passenger*, so it uses the Tethered Voice. **Q42** adds an open-sky exception: surviving orbital relays (*the moving lights*) carry the voice beyond its range on 9+ on a d12 per scene, and anyone with a receiver may overhear it. *Sends the Mind Abroad* stays open under Q30. *Sends the Mind Abroad* now uses the Relay-Sense (Q30), under the same open-sky rule.

**Recommendation.** Relay-Sense (A) for *Sends the Mind Abroad*, and Tethered Voice (B) for *Carries a Passenger*. Both keep every rule intact, and both give the player a reason to care about particular places and the people who control them, the way the kept specialists in D9 are tied to their compound.

## 5. Do Not Import (Clearance List)

Add these to the §10.3 clearance checklist. Don't use them as names, terms, or recognisable concepts:

- **Proper names:** Roland, Gilead, Mid-World, Arthur Eld / the Eld, Mejis, Hambry, Eluria, Tull, Lud, the Calla, Thunderclap, Devar-Toi, Algul Siento, Maerlyn, Walter, Flagg, the Crimson King, Mordred, Blaine, Shardik, Maturin.
- **Distinctive terms:** gunslinger (as an order name), ka, ka-tet, khef, sai, "thankee," "say true," "roont," "thinny," "todash," "stuffy guys," "Charyou tree," "Reap," "Dogan," "can-toi," "taheen," "Low Men," "Breakers," "Beams," "Guardians" (as a named set of twelve), "the Tower," "Wolves," "Little Sisters," "god-drums," "Pubes" / "Grays," "slow mutants," North Central Positronics, Type One / Two / Three.
- **Signature phrasing:** "the world has moved on"; "I do not aim with my hand"; "There are other worlds than these." Write your own lines for the same feelings.
- **Structural elements:** the Tower and its Beams as a reality-supporting system; travel between worlds; fate (ka) as a real force; a single overarching dark lord.
- **Specific images to change:** masked raiders on grey horses; one twin taken from each pair; a riddling monorail; a cyborg bear with a dish on its head; healing bugs called "doctors." Each adaptation above changes these.

## 6. Resolved Questions

All five were resolved on 2026-10-16 and logged in the design document's §13.

| # | Question | Ruling **[CANON]** | Design doc |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q37 | Do Long Levy returnees (D2) count as dhampirs? | **No.** They are a separate failed-conversion condition: strong, fast-growing, short-lived, often cognitively impaired, no weekly blood need. They carry a degraded maker-lineage tag. Folklore calls them dhampirs. NPC only. Levy mechanism option A adopted; option B retired | §5.12 |
| Q38 | Can a Digital Facsimile live in portable hardware? | **No.** Fixed-route machinery is allowed. Speech at a distance works only through relays within range of the home site. *Carries a Passenger* therefore uses the Tethered Voice | §5.15, Q30 |
| Q39 | Adopt the formal legal register (D13)? | **Yes, as political rule P7.** Binding instruments require the register; errors are grounds to challenge, not automatic voiding. Working name *charter-speech*; vocabulary built from scratch | §7.3, §7.6 |
| Q40 | Where does the Order of the Last Guns (D4) belong? | **A neighbouring province.** The March receives claimants, refugees, and relic sidearms; the order's history stays offstage | §8.2 |
| Q41 | Include the hunters' taxonomy (D8) as a player handout? | **Yes.** It goes in the Stalker chapter, cross-referenced in Occultist, written entirely in-world. Threat ranks remain the GM measure | §10.2 |

| Q42 | Can the Tethered Voice reach beyond range by satellite under open sky? | **Yes, intermittently.** The moving lights carry it on 9+ on a d12 per scene; it can be overheard; Truth 11 adds a watcher | §5.15 |

Q41 was amended afterwards: the classes are **Winterers**, **Strays** and **Tapsters**.

**Q30 resolved:** *Sends the Mind Abroad* uses the Relay-Sense (§4), with the same open-sky rule as the Tethered Voice: 9+ on a d12 per scene through the moving lights, with a risk of being noticed.
